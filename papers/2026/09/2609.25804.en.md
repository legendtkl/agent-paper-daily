## Conclusion summary

- Taste-Bench turns long agent trajectories into 502 decision forks. A model sees the shared prefix and two candidate directions but not the later outcome, then predicts which direction will end better.
- Labels come from parallel attempts or a detour followed by recovery. Human review of 100 questions yields 172 explicit A/B judgments, 170 supporting the mined label, or 98.8% agreement. This checks agreement with recorded outcomes, not global optimality.
- The best frontier model achieves only 59.7% order-robust accuracy. Forks whose decisive evidence appears later are harder, and larger reasoning budgets do not reliably improve accuracy.
- Distilling an outcome-aware teacher into an outcome-blind student raises unseen-task judgment accuracy from 30.0% to 47.9%.
- On 41 unseen SWE-bench Pro tasks, student advice raises a fixed executor from 14.6% to 33.7%; always-correct advice reaches 39.0%. The sample is small and labels come from observed trajectories rather than independent expert optimization.

## Research question and background

Long engineering and research tasks contain decisions about hypotheses, implementations, and experiments whose consequences emerge much later. End-state success cannot isolate whether the agent chose well at the critical fork, nor supply scalable step-level supervision.

The main contribution is an intermediate-decision benchmark (`EV`). Decision quality relates to planning (`RP`); teacher-student training uses executable outcomes (`AT`); the end-to-end test uses SWE-bench Pro (`SE`).

## Method

Parallel forks come from attempts with equivalent prefixes that diverge and later produce comparable outcomes. Detour forks come from a run that follows a direction, observes failure, and recovers through another. Generators propose forks; filters check prefix equivalence, outcome separation, causal plausibility, leakage, and non-triviality.

Each item contains the original task, trajectory prefix, and two choices while hiding future work. The primary metric requires correctness under both A/B orders. The 502 items cross software engineering versus ML research with parallel versus detour sources.

For training, a teacher sees the outcome and correct choice and writes a rationale. A student learns from these rationales without outcome access. End-to-end evaluation inserts student advice into the same executor on 41 unseen SWE-bench Pro tasks and compares no advice, student advice, and perfect advice.

## Experiments and evidence

The pipeline begins with 4,657 candidates and retains 502 after generation, triviality, and quality filters. Human reviewers inspect 100 items after seeing outcomes. Excluding judgments without a clear A/B preference, 170/172 support the mined label; 74 questions receive a clear consensus. This validates the recorded comparison but not counterfactual optimality.

Frontier models are evaluated with order-robust accuracy. The best reaches 59.7%, and performance falls when decisive evidence lies farther after the fork. More reasoning tokens do not consistently help and sometimes hurt.

On unseen tasks, the student's taste accuracy rises from 30.0% to 47.9%. With the same 41 tasks, executor, and advice format, success is 14.6% without advice, 39.0% with always-correct advice, and 33.7% with student advice. The student selects the supported direction on 77 of 98 realized forks.

## Limitations and risks

Observed trajectory outcomes are not independent expert solutions. Later execution quality, randomness, and environment state can still confound the fork; filtering reduces but cannot remove this. Human review sees the recorded outcomes and therefore checks label consistency rather than counterfactual optimality.

The benchmark covers only software engineering and ML research, and the end-to-end test has 41 tasks with one executor. Teacher rationales may transfer outcome artifacts or trajectory conventions. The strict two-order metric also marks any position sensitivity as failure, so one-order accuracy and bias should be reported together.

No independent rerun was found. Public code and data improve auditability, but votes, stars, and downloads do not validate the 14.6% to 33.7% result.

## Reproduction and application

The repository publishes generation, evaluation, training, and SWE-bench Pro advice code; the dataset exposes questions, labels, and metadata. Reproduction should pin trajectories, filter models, order protocol, teacher/student models, executor, and the 41-task list, and report retention, robust accuracy, and end-to-end success.

Applications include intermediate-decision diagnosis, trajectory mining, training-data construction, and runtime advice for research and coding agents. Production use should retain an end-state verifier, avoid treating historically better as universally better, and repeat human and counterfactual checks in new domains.

## Related work

- [Agent-as-a-Judge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10934) uses agents to evaluate agents; Taste-Bench freezes binary decisions from trajectory outcomes.
- [SWE-bench Pro](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16941) supplies difficult repository tasks; Taste-Bench tests advice on 41 of them.
- [MALT](https://metr.org/blog/2025-10-14-malt-dataset-of-natural-and-prompted-behaviors-that-threaten-eval-integrity) studies behavior in long trajectories; Taste-Bench extracts trainable decision forks.
- [RRSI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24972) improves harness evolution; Taste-Bench measures route choices inside one long run.
