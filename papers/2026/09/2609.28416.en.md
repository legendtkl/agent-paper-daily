## Conclusion summary

- AEWM predicts how an agent's proposed reasoning-action pair affects task progress instead of predicting high-entropy tool outputs. Action Judge labels decisions Critical, Exploratory, or Noisy; State Revision replaces Noisy continuations.
- Action Judge reaches 70.5% macro-F1 on 3,000 Search, Terminal, and software-engineering decisions, 10.6 points above the strongest frontier baseline. The absolute score still leaves meaningful online gating error.
- EditAct beats the strongest Best@3 baseline across six benchmarks and three Qwen3.5 agent backbones by 3.2–6.7 average points. A 9B agent with EditAct averages 44.1, above a 35B agent with plain ReAct at 42.2.
- AEWM-RFT trained on verified EditAct trajectories beats Self-RFT by 2.2–2.6 points without online AEWM guidance, indicating transferable supervision.
- The cross-domain evaluation, three runs, ablations, and open artifacts are strong. However, mid-training uses 52.16B tokens, online EditAct adds a judge call at each step plus revision calls, and end-to-end latency and cost are not fully reported.

## Research question and background

Language world models often predict the next search, terminal, or code-environment observation. Those outputs depend on live indexes, files, processes, and hidden state, while a real tool call will soon provide the actual answer. The paper reframes the bottleneck as task-state contamination: unsupported assumptions, stale plans, and wrong conclusions remain in history and distort later decisions.

AEWM changes the control and state-update structure, so `AF` is primary. Decision judgment and revision motivate `RP`; training on executed trajectories motivates `AT`; the judge benchmark and cross-domain evaluation motivate `EV`.

## Method

Action Judge reads task history and a proposed reasoning-action pair before execution and predicts Critical, Exploratory, or Noisy. Labels are generated retrospectively from successful verified trajectories, then filtered for future leakage, consistency, action validity, and reasoning grounded only in prefix-visible evidence.

When a proposal is Noisy, State Revision generates a new reasoning-action pair from the same observed history and executes it in the real environment. Samples are retained only when the revised action produces substantive progress. EditAct passes Critical and Exploratory proposals unchanged and replaces Noisy state, rather than appending a critique to a contaminated transcript.

Training combines 52.16B-token mid-training over Action Judge, State Revision, and interaction trajectories with SFT on 120,000 high-quality decision examples. AEWM-RFT trains the agent on verified EditAct trajectories so some correction behavior can be internalized without an online world model.

## Experiments and evidence

Evaluation covers BrowseComp, DeepSearchQA, Terminal-Bench 2.0, SWE-Bench Pro, Doc2Repo, and NL2Repo. DeepSearchQA and SWE-Bench Pro are out of distribution. Each benchmark is run independently three times. Agent backbones are Qwen3.5-4B, 9B, and 35B-A3B.

Action Judge reaches 70.5% overall macro-F1 and 60.9%, 72.1%, and 77.8% on Search, Terminal, and SWE, respectively. These are 10.3, 10.5, and 13.4 points above the strongest domain baselines.

Across six benchmarks, ReAct averages 28.5/34.5/42.2 for the 4B/9B/35B-A3B agents, while EditAct reaches 41.8/44.1/48.8. Relative to the strongest step-level or trajectory-level Best@3 baseline, gains are 6.7/5.2/3.2 points. With Qwen3.5-Plus, BrowseComp rises from 44.1% to 52.7%, while Terminal and SWE gains narrow, attributed to a capacity gap between AEWM and the stronger agent.

AEWM-RFT beats Self-RFT by 2.2, 2.6, and 2.5 points on BrowseComp, Terminal-Bench 2.0, and Doc2Repo. Turns decrease on BrowseComp and Terminal but rise to 89.8 on Doc2Repo, so quality gains do not imply uniform efficiency.

Ablations show Random Gate, Agent Resampling, AEWM Hint, reasoning-only revision, and action-only revision all trail full EditAct. Mid-training plus SFT beats either stage alone on three representative benchmarks, supporting the learned gate, joint direct editing, and two-stage recipe.

## Limitations and risks

Training labels are discovered retrospectively from successful or high-quality trajectories. Future-leakage filters help, but annotator preferences and successful-trajectory structure may still enter Action Judge. The 3,000-item judge benchmark is built through a related annotation pipeline and does not replace an independently hand-built failure set.

Online EditAct calls Action Judge at every non-final step and State Revision on Noisy steps. The paper reports task scores, turns, and some RFT efficiency, but not complete wall-clock, API cost, training compute, or a full same-budget search comparison. The 52.16B-token mid-training stage creates a high replication barrier.

Agent backbones all come from Qwen3.5, and AEWM itself is 35B-A3B. Cross-family transfer, closed-model agents, adversarial tools, and persistent long-term memory remain untested. AEWM is best understood as an agent-state regulator, not a model of environment dynamics.

## Reproduction and application

The official repository releases code and evaluation entrypoints; Hugging Face provides the AEWM model and Action Judge dataset under an Apache-2.0 code license. Appendices disclose task counts, step/time limits, context, temperature, generation budgets, scaffolds, and quality gates.

Full retraining still requires 52.16B tokens, internal deep-search data, CalibForge and DeNovoSWE trajectories, and multiple annotation and revision models. A practical first reproduction is to rerun Action Judge and three representative EditAct benchmarks from the public checkpoint, then measure added model calls, latency, and cost per successful task.

For high-authority actions, deployments should preserve the original proposal, revision rationale, and executed action so state editing does not become unauditable history rewriting.

## Related work

- [ReAct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629) interleaves reasoning and action; AEWM judges and replaces harmful continuations before execution.
- [Reflexion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366) writes language feedback into later trials; AEWM directly replaces current state so stale assumptions do not remain salient.
- [Self-Refine](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651) iteratively revises outputs; AEWM adds real execution, a learned gate, and cross-domain trajectory supervision.
- [AweAgent](https://github.com/AweAI-Team/AweAgent) is the public framework used to integrate EditAct with three domain scaffolds.
- [CliffCompaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26779) mechanically compresses long contexts; AEWM targets incorrect content rather than context length, making the mechanisms potentially complementary.
