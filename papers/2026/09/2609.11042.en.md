## Conclusion summary

- T1 starts from Qwen3.5-122B-A10B and uses PPO on real cloud-terminal sandboxes with task-native executable verifiers for trajectories exceeding 300 tool calls.
- TITO preserves sampled token IDs and R³ replays expert routing at every MoE layer, reducing the train-inference log-probability gap from 0.021 to 0.013 and eliminating token drift in the loss region.
- Under the same Terminus-2 harness, Terminal-Bench 2.1 rises from 43.8% base and 49.4% SFT to 64.0%; LHTB rises from 18.9/23.6 to 27.9, and Terminal-Bench Hard reaches 38.0%.
- The 122B model and two evaluation-trajectory datasets are public, and the report candidly covers failed runs, reward design, critic warm-up, asynchrony, and tail scheduling. Full training code, data, and cost accounting remain unavailable.
- There is no single-axis ablation: binary and dense-reward campaigns also change the pool, initialization, and routing replay, so the full gain cannot be assigned to dense reward alone.

## Research question and background

Long-horizon terminal-agent RL faces sparse credit from expensive trajectories and training-inference mismatch across tokenization, inference kernels, and discrete MoE routing. Small differences can cause the trainer to update a subnet that did not generate the sampled behavior.

## Method

Each task packages a long instruction, isolated environment, held-out verifier, and hidden reference solution. T1-15k selects 15,000 tasks from 37,484 recursively synthesized candidates using eight verifier, solution, instruction, and task-value dimensions, rejecting hidden requirements, leakage, shortcuts, and weak verifiers. Reward is the absolute number of passing assertions on a fixed global scale; a warm-started critic is updated at 30 times the canonical actor learning rate.

TITO preserves sampled token IDs rather than decoding and re-encoding outputs, and applies one offset map to prompts, completions, tool outputs, and loss masks. R³ records the 8-of-256 expert choices at each of 48 MoE layers and replays them during training. Rollout and training are one step asynchronous, and skim oversampling accepts early completions while cancelling the tail.

## Experiments and evidence

Evaluation covers 89 Terminal-Bench 2.1 tasks, 46 LHTB tasks, and 100 Terminal-Bench Hard tasks, with three attempts each. All use Harbor/Terminus-2, Daytona sandboxes, 96K context, at most 60 turns, a 3,600-second agent wall, and a 900-second verifier wall. Other-harness leaderboard rows are explicitly contextual only.

Binary reward from the base reaches 47.2% and does not beat the 49.4% SFT checkpoint. Dense reward on unfiltered RST-38k reaches 59.9%. The production combination of T1-15k, critic warm-up, TITO, and R³ reaches 64.0%. Under the same harness, Claude Opus 4.7 scores 66.1%, Opus 4.6 63.8%, and GPT-5.4 54.8%. The authors correctly note that T1 was trained for this harness, so this is not a universal model ranking.

LHTB improves from 18.9 base and 23.6 SFT to 27.9, a 4.3-point RL gain. Terminal-Bench Hard reaches 38.0%. The top five training categories make up 67.9% of the pool, and gains are uneven in underrepresented debugging, data-science, scientific-computing, and performance tasks.

## Limitations and risks

The paper lists eight limitations. Re-tokenized history still affects 2.6% of tokens; reward lacks a controlled single-axis ablation; verifiers run in agent-controlled sandboxes without read-only mounting or checksums; and skim oversampling removes the very long-tail tasks where evaluation failures concentrate. Data skew, limited use of full asynchrony, and missing optimizer, length-adaptive advantage, and synchronization records further constrain reproducibility.

The results cover one model family, one harness, and terminal benchmarks. Public weights and trajectories enable evaluation auditing, but missing training code, T1-15k, and a cost ledger prevent independent reproduction of 122B PPO. OOD means separation from benchmark seeds and tasks, not demonstrated coverage of production distributions.

## Reproduction and application

The lowest-cost audit is to inspect the public model and trajectories against verifier outputs, commands, turns, and scores. Full reproduction needs the 122B actor/critic, SGLang, slime, Megatron-Core, Daytona sandboxes, and large concurrent rollouts. A batch can consume hundreds of sandbox-hours; total accelerators, elapsed training time, and monetary cost are not reported.

TITO generalizes to token-faithful multi-turn agent RL, R³ to MoE training across different inference and training kernels, and fixed-scale assertion rewards to executable-verifier tasks. Production adaptations should add read-only verifiers, checksums, continuation of tail rollouts, and cross-harness evaluation.

## Research judgment

High reading priority and medium-high confidence for agent-RL, MoE-training, coding-agent, and terminal-evaluation teams. The 94 score reflects real execution rewards, three held-out suites, train-inference alignment mechanisms, public weights and trajectories, and unusually candid failure reporting. The 64.0% result is harness-specific; the more transferable contributions are token/routing fidelity, critic scheduling, and verifier-reward constraints. Watch for full training code, controlled reward ablations, cross-harness reruns, and verifier tamper resistance.
