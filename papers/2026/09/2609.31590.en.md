## Conclusion summary

- AgentWorld turns multi-agent collaboration from an aggregation of individual outputs into executable tasks requiring communication, division of labor, resource transfer, and plans maintained across rounds. It contains 100 human-authored tasks plus 100 augmented variants, with 3–20 asymmetric agents and horizons of up to roughly 55 rounds.
- Causal Collaboration Effectiveness (CCE) traces dependencies backward from successful actions and measures the fraction of actions on a causal path to success. It complements success rate but is not independent ground truth because an LLM judge still supplies the binary causal decisions.
- Gemini 3 Flash leads the four primary models on the main set with 52.0% strict success, 71.5% partial success, and 0.320 CCE; 27% of tasks are solved by no model. Claude Haiku 4.5 has lower aggregate success but higher collaboration efficiency conditional on success.
- Message volume is not monotonic with success. GPT-5 Mini averages 44.1 chats and 36.0% success, while Gemini averages 11.0 and leads. Stale or redundant communication, role confusion, factual errors, and premature completion dominate the communication failure analysis.
- The executable environment, tasks, results, and trajectory analysis are open. External validity beyond games, chat-only coordination, judge dependence, and a three-run check limited to one model on 35 tasks remain the main boundaries.

## Research question and background

Many multi-agent benchmarks use short interactions, competitive games, or aggregates of independent performance. They do not establish whether a team genuinely collaborates. Real coordination requires agents with private state to exchange incomplete information, preserve role assignments, respect resource dependencies, and maintain a shared plan. AgentWorld asks whether a black-box, long-horizon, role-asymmetric, executable environment can separately measure task completion and which actions actually enabled it.

The primary category is `EV` because the main contribution is an evaluation environment, task set, and metric. Mandatory inter-agent dependencies motivate `MA`; planning and communication over 25–55 rounds motivate `RP`.

## Method

The authors adapt Kaetram into a deterministic round-robin MMORPG sandbox supporting up to 1,000 concurrent agents, more than 380 items, 144 mob types, more than 70 NPCs, and 13 high-level API tools. High-level movement, gathering, combat, crafting, and trading tools suppress low-level control difficulty so the benchmark focuses on coordination and planning.

The 100 human-authored tasks span combat, crafting, gathering, trading, exploration, survival, construction, and coordination. Agents differ in skills, equipment, inventory, and spawn location; no single agent can complete a task alone. A 10% human audit of the 100 augmented variants finds preserved collaboration need, objective semantics, and role asymmetry in every sample, with appropriate difficulty in 90%.

In addition to strict success rate and partial success, CCE starts from verifier-confirmed success actions and traces dependencies backward round by round to build a Causal Action Graph. It divides contributing actions by all actions and assigns zero to failed tasks. Because the policy counts marginally helpful actions, CCE is best interpreted as an upper bound on genuinely useful effort.

## Experiments and evidence

On the 100-task main set, Gemini 3 Flash scores 52.0% SR, 71.5% PSR, and 0.320 CCE; Claude Haiku 4.5 scores 45.0%, 62.7%, and 0.294; GPT-5 Mini scores 36.0%, 62.6%, and 0.206; DeepSeek R1-70B scores 20.0%, 43.5%, and 0.125. The augmented set is harder: Gemini falls to 24.0% SR.

Gemini controls score 5.7% for random actions, 22.9% without communication, 28.6% with one agent, 52.0% under black-box multi-agent execution, and 60.0% when one oracle coordinator can see all messages. On a 35-task ablation subset, full AgentWorld scores 54.3%; removing asymmetric roles yields 45.7%, shortening the horizon 37.1%, and removing task documentation 29.4%.

CCE agrees with a human on 69 of 84 action labels, 82% raw agreement and Cohen's κ=0.64. GPT-4.1 and Claude Sonnet 4 agree at κ=0.66 over 7,298 decisions. Recomputing CCE with GPT-4.1, Claude Sonnet 4, and Llama-4 Maverick preserves the complete model ranking, although absolute values shift by up to about 0.05.

Only Gemini on 35 tasks receives a three-seed stability check. SR is 54.3% in every run, while average rounds are 18.77, 15.25, and 18.91. Identical aggregate SR does not imply identical task outcomes or statistical significance for the full model ranking.

## Limitations and risks

The paper explicitly limits its claims to one game-like sandbox with abstracted low-level control and chat as the only coordination channel. Failed tasks receive CCE zero, mechanically coupling CCE and SR, while an LLM judge labels causal links. Multi-seed evidence covers only one model and 35 tasks.

The sandbox isolates collaboration mechanisms but does not directly represent software repositories, approvals, or scientific experiments with irreversible effects, asynchronous execution, private channels, and dynamic permissions. The open repository contains tasks, results, evaluators, and analysis, but its last push at collection time was June 16, 2026; a frozen release, dependency lock, and total reproduction cost still need verification.

## Reproduction and application

The MPL-2.0 repository exposes tasks, agent code, evaluation outputs, verifiers, and analysis. Reproduction requires running Kaetram, configuring multiple proprietary or open models, and paying for long multi-agent trajectories. The paper does not report a complete dollar-cost ledger, so cost-effectiveness comparisons across models remain unresolved.

AgentWorld can test coordination protocols, shared plans, role allocation, communication budgets, and team memory. Its strongest transferable mechanism is to verify the terminal state deterministically and then trace actual dependencies, rather than treating message volume or holistic collaboration scores as evidence.

## Related work

- [MultiAgentBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01935) compares collaboration topologies and protocols; AgentWorld adds long horizons, black-box state, and asymmetric roles.
- [MindAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08155) evaluates collaboration in Overcooked; AgentWorld expands task, team, and world-level resource dependencies.
- [TheAgentCompany](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14161) evaluates agents in a simulated workplace; AgentWorld makes multiple independent agents jointly necessary.
- [Generative Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442) studies social simulation; AgentWorld adds deterministic verifiers and a quantitative collaboration metric.
- [Voyager](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291) studies long-horizon solo exploration in Minecraft; AgentWorld focuses on team coordination rather than individual skill accumulation.
