## Conclusion summary

- RRSI treats overfitting in harness self-improvement as search regularization. Proposal constraints limit bundled edits and encourage unexplored routes; selection constraints reject benchmark-specific, noisy, costly, or obsolete changes.
- Evaluation spans eight benchmarks in coding, agentic workspace, and engineering design. The largest gain is 14.1 points on the evolve split and 4.7 points on five out-of-distribution benchmarks.
- The final harness uses about 30% fewer policy tokens than unregularized evolution, so transfer is not purchased with higher policy inference volume.
- Ablations separate annealed edit budgets, evolution-history exploration, the critic, and the pruner, showing that proposal- and selection-side controls are complementary.
- Evidence comes from the authors' frozen models and environments. There is no complete independent rerun, and expensive external models and environments remain part of the total system cost.

## Research question and background

An agent harness includes prompts, control flow, tools, memory, and context management. Automated evolution can repeatedly select edits against a finite set, encoding benchmark patterns, chasing evaluation noise, and accumulating complexity. Large evolve-set gains may disappear on unseen tasks.

The work studies continued harness adaptation (`LT`), changes the agent architecture (`AF`), measures execution efficiency (`SY`), and relies on explicit evolve/OOD evaluation (`EV`).

## Method

RRSI leaves the harness editable but adds four controls. An annealed budget allows broader early changes and progressively limits the number of bundled edits. Evolution history exposes previous edits and failures to the proposer to reduce repetitive search.

A critic screens proposals for benchmark-specific text, verifiers, and other non-transferable information. A pruner removes changes that are too small, too expensive, or no longer useful. Candidates advance only after passing performance, cost, and specialization checks.

The backbone is frozen and improvements remain in readable harness components, enabling diff-level audits and transfer to tasks with different prompts, tools, and verifiers.

## Experiments and evidence

The study covers eight benchmarks in three domains and separates the evolve split from five OOD benchmarks. RRSI gains up to 14.1 points on evolve and 4.7 OOD. Several weaker evolution methods retain little gain, and some finish below the initial harness.

Comparisons include the initial harness, four baseline methods, RRSI, per-round curves, transfer tests, ablations, and policy-token accounting. OOD settings include SWE-bench Verified, JobBench/GDPval/APEX-Agents, and Frontier-Eng. Policy tokens fall about 30% relative to unregularized evolution.

Single controls are insufficient: an edit budget does not detect semantic leakage, while a critic alone does not remove accumulated complexity. Joint proposal and selection regularization gives the strongest transfer-efficiency trade-off. Released harnesses and evolution logs make benchmark-specific edits inspectable.

## Limitations and risks

The evolve sets, frozen policy models, and harness representation remain specific. Three domains and eight benchmarks do not establish transfer to every digital, GUI, or embodied agent. External model and environment costs are not reduced to one total-cost measure; 30% fewer policy tokens is not 30% lower total evolution cost.

Research inference: a model-based critic may miss semantic benchmark specialization, while a pruner can remove abilities whose value appears only later. Harness-level recursive improvement is not autonomous improvement of model weights, objectives, or safety boundaries.

Public code improves reproducibility, but no third party was found rerunning all eight benchmarks. Stars and votes show attention, not validation.

## Reproduction and application

The Apache-2.0 repository includes RRSI, domain adapters, tests, and evaluation scripts. Reproduction should pin the backbone, initial harness, evolve/evaluation split, call budget, concurrency, and verifier, while recording every candidate, rejection reason, policy tokens, and OOD result.

Applications include continuous improvement of coding, research, and enterprise workflow harnesses. Production use should isolate evolution tasks from promotion tests, freeze hidden regressions, cap per-round changes, preserve rollback versions, and never deploy based only on evolve scores.
