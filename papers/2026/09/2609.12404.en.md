## Conclusion summary

- VRL-Bench compares cross-trial learning with retry under the same first failure, complete-trial budget, and interaction opportunities.
- Reflexion, DC-Cu, and ACE each outperform memory-free retry in some model-environment settings and underperform it in others.
- VEX² jointly selects policies and allocates remaining trials. All six point estimates are positive, but two GLM confidence intervals cross zero.
- Paired inference and 3,324 replay-exposure episodes strengthen the evidence, while coverage remains limited to three models, two primary environments, and six trials.

## Research question and background

Failure reflections can preserve useful experience or anchor an agent to the wrong repair. Without a memory-free retry under equal budget, gains may come from another chance rather than learning. VRL-Bench standardizes cross-trial information, resets, and complete-trial budgets.

## Method

Each task allows at most six complete trials and stops at success. Conditions share the same first failed trial, then reset to the same external initial state with equal remaining opportunities. Retry carries no failure-derived text; the other methods retain their original updates.

VEX² builds an evidence-grounded semantic policy tree, allocates all remaining complete trials across policy leaves, and selects the next policy. It does not search restorable intermediate states, and the runtime forwards the scheduler response to the Actor without parsing or repair.

## Experiments and evidence

The primary matrix covers DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GPT-5.4 nano, and GLM-4.7-FlashX on MiniWoB and WebShop. MiniWoB has 64 task families and 640 paired cases; WebShop uses 100 goals. AlfWorld and HotPotQA add 3,324 replay-exposure episodes.

Reflexion raises GPT-5.4 nano WebShop from 39% retry to 55%, with a 95% interval of [+8,+25], but lowers GLM-4.7-FlashX from 50% to 46%; ACE reaches 45%. VEX² gains +6.6, +4.5, and +1.6 points on MiniWoB and +16, +17, and +8 on WebShop. The GLM intervals [-1.1,+4.4] and [-1,+17] cross zero.

## Limitations and risks

The paper explicitly limits claims to three models, two primary environments, six-trial budgets, and visible feedback. Complete trials and interactions are matched, not model calls or tokens. Irreversible actions, hidden feedback, other budgets, and reset semantics remain open. VEX² also uses one three-shot prompt and same-family scheduler/Actor profiles, so cross-model scheduling and cost gains are unproven.

## Reproduction and application

The CC BY 4.0 source supplement contains prompts, the runtime loop, paired statistics, resource accounting, code, and data. Full reproduction still needs four environments and model APIs. A practical deployment should first compare any reflection policy with memory-free retry under equal trial and interaction budgets.
