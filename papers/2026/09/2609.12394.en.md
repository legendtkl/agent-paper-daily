## Conclusion summary

- BlueLM-GUI connects real-device data production, CPT, SFT, online RL, and evaluation into one executable training loop.
- It scores 87.4% on MobileGUI-VBench, 5.1 points above the best closed API baseline, and 84.9% on AndroidWorld versus the top 85.3% result.
- From one SFT checkpoint, offline RL scores 73.9% and real-device online RL 82.0%; atomic five-pass judging raises 72.52% to 77.42%.
- The benchmark and evaluation code are open, but the model, training corpus, device infrastructure, and full cost are not reproducible.

## Research question and background

End-to-end mobile agents are increasingly limited by training-distribution mismatch, discarded failure trajectories, and saturated benchmarks. Sandboxes miss account state, risk controls, ads, network failures, and application fragmentation, while static benchmarks stop diagnosing progress.

## Method

Starting from Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, the system runs CPT, SFT, then GRPO. CPT uses 15B tokens, roughly 70% from real-device GUI trajectories; SFT uses 5B tokens of expert-verified trajectories. RL runs on hundreds of phones and Android emulators with eight instructions and eight trajectories per instruction per iteration.

HTSC requires agreement among three heterogeneous evaluators and routes disagreements to human adjudication. ECDM converts failures into step correction, retrospective query alignment, or counterfactual tasks. MobileGUI-VBench uses explicit quotas over scenario, complexity, interaction, and risk dimensions.

## Experiments and evidence

MobileGUI-VBench contains 150 tasks over 40 applications and eight scenario classes. BlueLM-GUI scores 87.4%, Seed2.1-Pro 82.3%, and the strongest open baseline GLM5.3-Flash 66.4%. AndroidWorld scores are 84.9% for BlueLM-GUI, 85.3% for Qwen3.8-Max, and 84.5% for Qwen3.8-Flash.

Base, Base + SFT, and Base + CPT + SFT score 36.6%, 64.1%, and 70.8% on MobileGUI-VBench-Basic. From one 74.9% SFT checkpoint, offline RL reaches 73.9% and online real-device RL 82.0%. Replacing one holistic reward judgment with frame-bound atomic conditions and five passes raises 72.52% to 77.42%.

## Limitations and risks

The authors note that real-device collection is expensive, hard to reset, and exposed to expired logins, redesigns, and network errors. The same team built the model, benchmark, and evaluation, leaving adaptation risk. No total training cost, repeated-run uncertainty, or confidence intervals are reported. Without weights, the full corpus, and the RL environment, the 87.4% result cannot be independently rerun.

## Reproduction and application

The public scope supports reproducing the benchmark distribution and AndroidWorld protocol, not the training loop or model score. The quick start still needs a BlueLM-GUI service, emulators, and a task-data API. Transferable ideas include real state feedback, failure salvage, and atomic reward verification.
