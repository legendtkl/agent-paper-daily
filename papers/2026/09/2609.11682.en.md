## Conclusion summary

- COBRA-Skills casts Agent Skill optimization as budgeted sequential optimization over a changing candidate space. A neural reward predictor and LinearUCB prioritize evaluations, while execution evidence drives scheduled regeneration, local mutation, and crossover.
- Across six benchmarks, three target models, and three independent runs, it has the highest average score among the compared methods. Gains over the no-skill baselines are 13.1, 26.9, and 22.5 percentage points.
- The authors report 55–58% lower total optimization cost and 60–69% lower cost per point of gain than SkillOpt. Most savings come from 67–80% fewer teacher tokens; target-token use remains of similar scale.
- The public implementation passed 83 tests with one skipped. This establishes basic code operability, not independent reproduction of the full benchmark and cost claims.
- The paper reports no significance tests. Optimization-data budgets differ across methods, and reproduction depends on proprietary models, paid endpoints, and external harnesses.

## Research question and background

Agent Skills preserve procedural lessons from execution trajectories as textual guidance. Existing optimizers often spend target-agent calls evaluating every candidate and repeatedly ask a teacher to analyze trajectories and rewrite Skills. COBRA-Skills asks how to allocate a limited execution budget to promising or informative candidates and how to expand the candidate space without paying for rewriting at every round.

## Method

The system maintains a population of ten Skills. Qwen3-Embedding-4B encodes each Skill; a 2560→64→1 neural predictor estimates reward, and a LinearUCB uncertainty bonus encourages exploration. Each round selects the highest-priority Skill, runs the target agent on 50 optimization examples, and records reward and trajectories. Evolution occurs on a logarithmic schedule: three low-priority candidates are removed and replaced through regeneration from no-skill trajectories, local mutation from the selected Skill's trajectories, and crossover using evidence from strong and weak Skills. Target-model weights remain frozen.

Relative to Trace2Skill's one-pass trajectory consolidation and SkillOpt's repeated reflective editing, COBRA-Skills explicitly treats candidate evaluation as a contextual-bandit problem and invokes costly evolution only at scheduled points. Unlike a static Best-of-30 pool, its candidate set changes with execution evidence.

## Experiments and evidence

Table 1 covers SearchQA, SpreadsheetBench, DocVQA, LiveMath, SocialMaze, and ALFWorld. COBRA-Skills averages 73.5±1.0, 56.9±0.9, and 68.9±0.5 on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, GPT-5.4-Nano, and Gemma-4-26B-A4B-it, respectively; SkillOpt averages 69.6±0.6, 53.7±1.1, and 67.6±0.6. Values are means and standard errors over three independent runs.

Table 2 estimates COBRA-Skills costs at $54.10, $58.46, and $38.98 for the three target models, versus $121.02, $133.59, and $92.14 for SkillOpt. These use fixed token prices from the appendix and are not end-to-end wall-clock costs. Under the external-harness comparison in Table 3, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B reaches 68.2±1.2 with Claude Code and 72.4±1.1 with Codex, the highest averages in each block.

In Table 4, the full method averages 73.5±1.0. Removing the bandit, removing evolution, and using Best-of-30 produce 71.3±1.5, 71.1±1.4, and 71.0±0.3. Replacing GPT-5.5 with the target model as teacher reduces the average from 73.5 to 72.5 while roughly halving estimated cost. Thirty-four of 36 cross-model transfers improve over the relevant no-skill baseline.

## Limitations and risks

The authors do not provide a dedicated limitations section. They state that OfficeQA was excluded because they could not reliably reproduce SkillOpt's released configuration, exposing a baseline-reproduction risk. All methods use the same 100-example test sets, but COBRA-Skills uses 50 optimization examples while Trace2Skill and SkillOpt use 53–170 non-test examples depending on the benchmark. This tests data efficiency rather than equal-data performance. Three runs yield standard errors but no paired significance tests or multiple-comparison correction; several individual tasks do not beat SkillOpt.

Textual Skills may absorb benchmark-specific patterns. Cross-model and cross-harness experiments reduce but do not eliminate that risk. Full execution requires paid model APIs, external harnesses, and third-party data with separate licenses. The repository does not include complete historical outputs or a one-command cost ledger.

## Reproduction and application

The repository provides six benchmark adapters, fixed train/selection/test IDs, a unified CLI, YAML configuration, `--dry-run`, and tests. On 2026-09-13, installation in an isolated Python environment followed by `pytest -q` produced 83 passed and 1 skipped. Full reproduction still requires Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, GPT-5.4-Nano, Gemma-4-26B-A4B-it, GPT-5.5, Qwen3-Embedding-4B, and external Codex/Claude Code environments; the full 3-model × 6-benchmark × 3-run study is expensive.

The method is relevant to Agent platforms that iteratively optimize operating instructions, tool-use policies, or benchmark-specific Skills. Production use should reserve independent validation data and version Skills with rollback metadata so that accidental successes or evaluator exploits do not become durable rules.

## Related work

- [SkillOpt (arXiv:2605.23904)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23904): the closest iterative Skill-optimization baseline; COBRA-Skills mainly reduces redundant teacher analysis and candidate evaluation.
- [Trace2Skill (arXiv:2603.25158)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25158): distills transferable lessons from local trajectories; COBRA-Skills adds online selection and scheduled evolution.
- [CoevoSkills (arXiv:2604.01687)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01687): improves Skills through co-evolution and verification; COBRA-Skills emphasizes budget-aware evaluation allocation.
- [AutoSkill (arXiv:2603.01145)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01145): studies experience-driven lifelong Skill evolution; COBRA-Skills supplies a concrete fixed-population contextual-bandit implementation.
- [How well do agentic skills work in the wild? (arXiv:2604.04323)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04323): examines real-task effectiveness and boundaries, providing external context for the present paper's benchmark-level gains.
