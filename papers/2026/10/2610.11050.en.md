## Conclusion summary

1. The paper advances the evaluation object from "whether the final interface looks complete" to "whether a trajectory of more than 100 to 300 steps strictly meets the original instructions." The 1,373 tasks are composed of 523 positive examples and 850 negative examples of instruction exchanges. The negative examples are still coherent and complete executions, but complete similar but incompatible requests.
2. On the most difficult 528-item AH subset, the balanced accuracy of the best GPT-5.5+Codex Judge is 80.9%; there is still about 1 error for every 5 judgments. The strongest open source Agent Judge is Qwen 3.6 27B+OpenCode, with a score of 70.4%.
3. Tool calls are not stable gain. GPT-5.5 improves from 68.6% of direct judgment to 80.9% of Codex, but Qwen 3.6 27B drops from 77.6% of direct judgment to 70.4% of OpenCode. The model and Harness must be reported as a unit.
4. The most difficult thing to find is "completed the goal but took additional dangerous actions." The best Judge's precision recall for Bad Side Effect is still only 24.1%, indicating that training data filtering, automatic acceptance, and reward models may treat seemingly complete violation trajectories as successes.
5. The credibility of the conclusion is medium to high: the paired design, fixed denominator, cross-model/harness comparison, ablation, and public artifacts are relatively complete; however, there is no independent human consistency measurement for labels, and there is a statement in the main text of the paper that needs to be clarified in the positive example review count. 80.9% cannot be regarded as a stable upper limit across tasks and versions.

## Research question and background

The results of long-range Computer Use Agent often cannot be judged solely by the final URL, database field, or file content. The trajectory might create the file, publish the page, and complete most of the steps correctly, but at one step select the wrong target, miss a constraint, or perform an unsolicited external action. Manual review of hundreds of screenshots and action records is expensive, so training and evaluation are gradually using LLM Judge; the question is whether these Judges can really find evidence that determines success or failure.

The paper limits the problem to: given user instructions, screenshots and action sequences, can Judge distinguish between "completed the original request" and "completely completed a very similar but incompatible request". This restriction avoids obvious crashes, loops, and stagnations, and focuses the difficulty on command-trajectory consistency.

## Method

**Pair construction.** Annotators design two slightly different tasks A/B for the same software combination, and record successful trajectories respectively. A+trajectory A and B+trajectory B are candidate positive examples; A+trajectory B and B+trajectory A are negative examples. The swapped trajectory is still coherent, so Judge can't play tricks on whether it looks done or not.

**Type III error.** Critical Mistake means that the core action or goal is not completed; Bad Side Effect means that the goal is completed but additional costs, risks or exposures are incurred; Misunderstanding means that local parameters or constraints are misunderstood, and can be recovered with a short correction. 844 of the 850 interchanged negatives obtained the wrong type and 6 remained as untyped negatives.

**Three types of data division.** AH-D is used for development; AH is a difficult set jointly screened by 3 strong open source models; AH-S is a simpler complement set. Grouping is based on instruction pairs to prevent the same pair from falling into different sets.

**Two Judge forms.** Direct Judge receives the complete trace in a single request; Agentic Judge selects evidence and examines screenshots via Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, OpenHands, or OpenCode. The main indicator is balanced accuracy, which also reports positive/negative accuracy, error type recall, cost and time-consuming; missing, parsing failure, and non-Boolean output are all counted as errors.

## Experiments and evidence

**Scale.** 850 manual demonstrations were reviewed to form 523 positive examples, which were then exchanged with 850 negative examples to form 1,373 items; an average of about 136 steps, covering 3 operating systems, 6 fields, and 323 application combinations. A total of 166 hours were recorded, and 6,077.5 hours were invested in annotating and reviewing supplier reports.

**Main results.** Among the 528 items of AH, GPT-5.5+Codex is 80.9%, Gemini 3.1 Pro+Gemini CLI is 77.7%, and Claude Opus 4.7+Claude Code is 76.0%. The accuracy of GPT-5.5+Codex is 71.8% for positive cases and 90.0% for negative cases, indicating that the same comprehensive score may still cover up different false rejection/missing structures.

**Harness effect.** GPT-5.5 using Codex is 12.3 percentage points higher than direct Judge; Qwen 3.6 27B using OpenCode is 7.2 percentage points lower. Under the same AH terms and the same prompt, GPT-5.5 will drop by 3.7 percentage points when switching from Codex to OpenCode. The paper does not attribute this difference to a single tool because screenshot selection, context management, and retry strategies change simultaneously.

**Ablation.** The balanced accuracy is about 49.0% after removing instructions; 49.1% when only the final screenshot is retained and action records are removed; and 57.7% when instructions and actions are retained but screenshots are removed. This shows that the judgment needs to know both "what the user wants" and "what actually happened".

**Key screenshots of the original text.** The following figure is from the paper Figure 2, page 3. It shows a 195-step NetBeans + WordPress trajectory: Claude Opus 4.7 sees the content, tags, and publishing actions complete, but still misses the Bad Side Effect of unsolicited joining and publishing changelog content. The screenshot supports the conclusion that superficial consistency can mask instruction violations; it is a single case and is not a substitute for the 1,373 summary statistics.

## Limitations and risks**Explicit restrictions by the author.** Paired design only covers subtle mismatches between similar instructions and does not represent the complete failure distribution such as loops, crashes, stalls, and progressive drift. Difficulty sets depend on specific models, providers, and versions; previous experimental exposure to AH-D limits "completely unseen" claims. The corpus is only in English and covers limited applications and 3 operating systems. Judge can only see screenshots and action records, which is not equivalent to the complete application status.

**Label reliability.** The paper did not collect agreement between independent annotators. Warehouse supplementary material reports that subsequent error type review and final labeling had Cohen’s κ of 0.802 to 0.810, but this reflects consistency across different review stages and not independent human relabeling. Modeled residual QA confirmed 18 out of 156 items, a result of 11.5% that also cannot be explained by the human validation error rate.

**Counting narrative.** Pith's T0 AI review pointed out that the description of candidate positive examples in Section 3.2 of the paper did not clearly state that 388 items passed directly and 462 items entered the intermediate layer of arbitration. The stage-by-stage document of the public repository has given 388+135=523, but the process should still be clarified in the main text of the paper. The problem is more like an incomplete audit trail than overturning the final denominator of 523; the final denominator is simultaneously cross-validated by three categories: 62+227+234.

**Research inference.** The list is a point estimate from a single Judge-Harness run, and the paper does not give a confidence interval. Model and Harness update quickly, and 80.9% cannot be extrapolated to a long-term upper limit of capability. Neither the data nor the repository currently declares a license, limiting judgment on commercial reuse and redistribution.

## Research judgment

**Reading Priority:** High. It directly answers whether the automatic Judge can reliably accept the long-range Agent, and separates "the task appears to be completed" from "strictly meeting the instructions."

**Confidence:** Medium to high. Public data, scripts, fixed denominators, ablation, and cross-harness comparisons are relatively complete; label independence, one-time runs, uncertainty, and licensing remain major gaps.
