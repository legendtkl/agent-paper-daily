## Conclusion summary

1. Self-HPO puts the Agent's Harness search and policy reinforcement learning into the same alternating cycle: the current policy first analyzes the failure and rewrites the Harness, and then trains under the new Harness; the unsolved tasks become the next round of Harness search set.
2. On Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, the average solution rate of the three Coding Agent benchmarks increased from 57.8% to 64.8%; SWE-Bench Verified, Multilingual, and Pro increased by 8.5, 5.5, and 6.9 percentage points respectively.
3. Controlled experiments support "joint optimization is better than unilateral optimization": the average score of Self-HPO is 2.9 points higher than RLVR that only trains the strategy, and 4.7 points higher than Self-Harness that only optimizes Harness.
4. The evidence boundary is clear: the results come from a base model, a mini-SWE-agent family and two rounds of optimization; a complete reproduction requires 8 B200s, a remote Orchard sandbox and large-scale trajectory generation, and there is no independent rerun.

## Research question and background

The results of Coding Agent are affected by both the strategy model and Harness. Fixed Harness will limit the trajectories that the strategy can see and generate; fixed strategy will make the Harness search continue to target the old capability boundaries. Existing joint optimization methods usually rewrite Harness from a frozen external strong model, and cannot answer "whether the strategy being trained can become the rewriter of the next round of Harness."

## Method

**H-step:** Fix the current strategy, read the failure trajectories on the search set, generate 6 Harness candidates, and select the best version based on the executable verification results. The first round search set randomly selected 300 tasks from the training data; subsequent search sets were changed to tasks that were never solved in the previous round of policy training.

**P-step:** Collect on-policy trajectories under the new Harness to test whether it passes as a binary reward, update the policy with GRPO and Balanced Adaptive Rollout. Each task samples up to 8 trajectories and constructs 4 reward balancing groups; 50 policy updates are performed per round.

**Recursive relationship:** The updated policy is both the next round task solver and the next round Harness proposer. Compared with Self-Harness, it changes the policy simultaneously; compared with HPO, it does not rely on Opus 4.8 as an external proposer; compared with WHALE and HarnessForge, the Harness search set changes dynamically with the remaining failures of the policy.

## Experiments and evidence

**Settings:** The training set contains 2,484 tasks from SWE-rebench, SWE-rebench V2 and Scale-SWE; the evaluation covers 500 questions from SWE-Bench Verified, 300 questions from SWE-Bench Multilingual and 642 questions from the SWE-Bench Pro V2 public set. The main results are the mean and standard deviation of 3 runs.

| Methods | SWE-Verified | SWE-Multi | SWE-Pro | Average |
|-------------|--------------|------------|------------|------------|
| Base | 61.0 ± 0.2 | 54.2 ± 0.3 | 58.3 ± 1.2 | 57.8 ± 0.4 |
| RLVR | 67.5 ± 0.8 | 56.3 ± 1.0 | 61.8 ± 1.3 | 61.9 ± 0.6 |
| Self-Harness | 65.2 ± 1.5 | 54.3 ± 0.7 | 60.7 ± 0.9 | 60.1 ± 0.6 |
| Self-HPO | 69.5 ± 0.9 | 59.7 ± 0.8 | 65.2 ± 1.4 | 64.8 ± 0.6 |

**Ablation:** In the HPO settings of the external proposer, changing RL to SFT or RFT, SWE-Verified dropped from 71.6 to 67.2 and 70.5 respectively; fixing the second round search set dropped to 70.1; using the current strategy to self-propose to get 69.5. The cross combination shows that the final Harness with the basic strategy is 64.5, the final strategy with the initial Harness is 64.4, and the combination of the two is 69.5. The joint gain exceeds the sum of the two separate gains by 1.6 points.

**Reliability:** Under 8 samples, pass@8 increases from 80.0% to 84.8%. The pass@1 of medium and easy tasks increased by 17.2 and 12.9 points respectively, and the always-failed task increased by 6.1 points; however, the base always-successful task dropped by 2.2 points, indicating that the improvement is not monotonous on a task-by-task basis.

## Limitations and risks

**Limitations directly supported by the author's evidence:** Only Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and mini-SWE-agent are verified; training only uses Python projects. Although there is a gain in cross-language testing, it cannot be extrapolated to Web, GUI or embodied Agent. Self-HPO is still 2.1 points lower than HPO using the Opus 4.8 proposer.

**Research inference:** The search set and policy training share the same training pool, and Harness may learn the SWE-style submission protocol and verifier features. Although the three tests cover foreign languages ​​and warehouses, they are still similar software repair tasks and cannot prove long-term self-improvement in an open environment. The empty patching, interface checking and submission repair mechanisms added by Harness have clear engineering value, and may mainly fix baseline protocol failures rather than improve general reasoning.

## Related work- [Self-Harness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09498): The strategy is not updated, and only the fixed strategy is allowed to rewrite Harness. This is the most direct one-sided comparison in this article.
- [Meta-Harness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28052): Use an external strong model to search for Harness; this article compares the two settings of external proposer and self-proposal.
- [WHALE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00196): Jointly optimize Harness and model weights, but keep Harness proposer and search set fixed.
- [HarnessForge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01779): Study the joint evolution of Harness and strategy, still relying on external models to rewrite Harness.
- [SWE-agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15793): Taking the Agent-computer interface as an important variable in software repair performance forms the basis of Harness research.
