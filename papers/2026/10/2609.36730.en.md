## Conclusion summary

- The paper defines library quality by whether downstream agents produce correct and simple programs, not by whether the library compiles, passes its own tests, or appeals to human reviewers.
- LibraryDesignBench covers 242 expert-validated problems, 15 library-design tasks, and four languages. Each designer setup produces three libraries evaluated by three implementer agents, totaling 2,178 trials per setup.
- Opus 5.5 scores 48.9, slightly above the human production-library reference at 46.6. Test-pass rates, however, cluster between 84.1% and 86.6%; residual program complexity drives most ranking differences.
- Designers reproduce the production abstraction in 11 of 15 tasks. In an audit of 810 partially passing, overlong solutions, 82% of primary causes are library limitations; rigidity and verbosity account for 64%, while missing capability accounts for 14%.
- A combined agent-first intervention raises GPT-6 Astra from 44.1 to 46.4 but nearly doubles design cost from USD 4.51 to USD 8.24. The paper does not isolate the contribution of each intervention component.

## Research question and background

Coding-agent benchmarks usually test whether generated implementations pass tests. They rarely ask whether a library generated by one agent reduces work for later agents. Internal tests cannot measure discoverability, composability, or flexibility; prescribed API grading leaks the desired design; and human review may reward interfaces optimized for people rather than models.

The paper separates designers from users. The designer sees a capability brief and examples but no API, method signatures, or tests. Downstream implementers then solve hidden-test problems with the library. Quality is the correctness and remaining complexity of their programs.

## Method

Each task has a design phase and an evaluation phase. The benchmark spans Rust, Python, TypeScript, and Haskell across 15 library families. A downstream solution scores squared test-pass rate multiplied by simplicity. Simplicity averages ratios against expert references for source lines of code, cyclomatic complexity, cognitive complexity, and Halstead volume, each capped at one.

Production-library and no-library conditions are fixed references. Eleven model–harness designer setups cover nine frontier models; three implementers come from different model families. Containers, prompts, formatters, hidden tests, and task weights are held fixed. Confidence intervals are clustered approximately at the task level.

## Experiments and evidence

Table 1 reports Opus 5.5 at 48.9 [48.0, 49.9], the production library at 46.6 [46.1, 47.2], and no library at 34.4 [33.9, 34.9]. DeepSeek V4 Pro scores 31.2, below no library. In Haskell, agent-written libraries underperform no library for roughly 70% of designer–task pairs.

Pass rates vary only from 84.1% to 86.6%. Production libraries add 12.3±0.4 points over no library at USD 0.14±0.02 more per problem. Opus 5.5 reaches 86.6% pass and 64.5 simplicity; production libraries reach 85.4% and 61.5.

The failure audit covers 810 samples from six designer configurations. Library limitations explain 82% of primary excess-code causes; rigidity and verbosity contribute 64%, versus 14% for coverage. A separate author analysis finds that 41% of 13,562 hand-labeled reimplementation lines duplicate functionality already present but hidden, awkward, or broken.

LibraryUseBench fixes the production library. Raising GPT-5.6 Luna reasoning effort increases pass rate from 51.4% to 84.5% and files read from 2 to 15. More prescriptive prompts mainly improve simplicity from 48.7 to 60.4. With the minimal prompt, 24% of runs never open the library.

The combined agent-first prompt, runnable examples, and subagent testing raise Astra from 44.1 to 46.4, improving all implementers and three of four languages. Most of the gain comes from shorter programs, while design cost nearly doubles.

## Limitations and risks

The authors note that the 15 tasks favor domains with mature production libraries and clear reuse opportunities. Static simplicity is a proxy: it does not cover runtime performance, long-term maintenance, security, compatibility, or human comprehension. Model and harness choices are fixed.

Our inference is that expert references based on production libraries may implicitly privilege existing API designs. Static complexity measures only indirectly approximate agent reasoning cost. The 2.3-point mean lead over production does not come with a direct paired-difference interval, so it is not evidence of a stable general win. Model versions and prices will also drift.

## Reproduction and application

The repository releases tasks, configurations, runners, result tooling, documentation, and an Apache-2.0 license. At commit `905bb62859cf5ae340562bf4bde6abfa1b9f5a41`, the local test run produced 212 passed and 8 skipped; all skipped tests are opt-in slow end-to-end tests. The 2,178 model trials per setup and cost ledger were not rerun.

Engineering reviews can add downstream-agent residual-work metrics: hidden-test pass rate, new code volume, documentation access, duplicated functionality, tool calls, and cost. Consumer-first sketches, runnable examples, and subagent trials are plausible interventions, but they should be ablated separately.

## Related work
