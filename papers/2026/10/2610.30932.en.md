## Conclusion summary

- UserArena turns 64,729 real e-commerce sessions into an interactive environment and evaluates output validity, local actions, free-running trajectories, personalized cohorts, and terminal outcomes through a five-level protocol.
- OPTD exposes a frozen teacher to high-level intent extracted from the complete training trajectory, distills token-level distributions on the student's own responses, and constrains training-only intent through a trust region.
- With a controlled Qwen3.5-2B backbone, OPTD leads all eight single-session L1–L4 metrics and five of eight cross-session metrics, placing second on two more. Some margins are small, and no repeated-run variance is reported.
- For one production ranking-policy pair, OPTD estimates 0.713 times the online PV-CTR effect. It is the closest tested simulator but still underestimates the effect by 28.7%, and the validation covers only one policy pair and one metric.
- The repository exposes enough to inspect the environment and evaluator, but only 535 held-out sessions are public. Full data, checkpoints, and primary experiment traces are absent, so the main table is not independently reproducible.

## Research question and background

Agentic recommendation adapts decisions over multi-step interactions. Next-action accuracy alone cannot establish that a simulator remains realistic when it runs freely, preserves differences across user groups, or reproduces final conversion behavior. Prior benchmarks also rarely test whether a simulated policy effect agrees with a controlled production A/B result.

UserArena puts what users saw, how they acted, and how their preferences evolved across visits into one executable evaluation. It also proposes a training method that supplies long-horizon guidance on student-generated prefixes.

## Method

The benchmark reconstructs recommendation feeds and item-detail pages with thirteen action types. Single-session evaluation uses replay from logged states and free rollout; cross-session evaluation connects visits from the same user in chronological order. L0 checks parseable, executable output; L1 checks local actions; L2 checks decision distributions and trajectory length; L3 checks cohort-level behavioral differences; and L4 checks click, cart, and terminal-state outcomes.

OPTD extracts a high-level intent record from the full training trajectory while excluding concrete products, actions, prices, and outcomes. The student generates a response at a real state. A frozen teacher then scores every token both with and without intent. Log-probability interpolation and a KL budget cap the effect of privileged intent. The final objective combines token-level JSD distillation with supervised grounding on recorded responses. Intent is teacher-only during training and is unavailable to the deployed student.

## Experiments and evidence

The paper reports 11,010 users, 64,729 sessions, 577,670 actions, 566,102 products, and thirteen action types. The cross-session subset contains 5,768 users and 59,378 sessions, averaging 10.29 sessions and 86.36 actions per user. Main experiments use Qwen3.5-2B for six text simulators, two GUI simulators, and OPTD.

In the single-session setting, OPTD records macro-F1/accuracy of 0.4178/0.3204, DM-JSD of 0.0156, trajectory error of 1.72, and population fidelity of 0.8692; it leads all eight L1–L4 metrics. Cross-session values are 0.3456/0.2969, 0.0284, 0.72, and 0.8929, respectively; OPTD leads five metrics and places second on two. STA remains better on macro-F1 and CTR error.

The production comparison fixes 10,000 users, 5,000 per policy arm. OPTD estimates 0.713 times the production PV-CTR difference, for an effect error of 0.287; the next closest task-specific simulator, STA, reaches 0.297 times. GPT-6-Sol and GPT-6-Luna overestimate the effect at 1.576 and 2.154 times, showing that model scale alone does not calibrate behavior.

Every component ablation hurts at least one representative metric, but the paper reports only a relative figure rather than absolute values. Human evaluation uses 200 trajectory pairs per simulator and three annotators. OPTD has a 38.7% correct machine-identification rate, a 26.3% real-as-machine error rate, and Fleiss' kappa of 0.528.

## Limitations and risks

The authors acknowledge that every simulator differs from production, with OPTD still underestimating the effect by 28.7%. Long-horizon preference evolution and cross-session consistency remain open. The data are restricted to non-commercial research; re-identification, external personal-data linkage, and unauthorized redistribution are prohibited.

Research judgment: one policy pair and one metric cannot establish reliable ranking among many policies. The main table has no confidence intervals or repeated-run variance, and OPTD exceeds Customer-R1 by only 0.007 macro-F1 in the single-session setting. A common 2B backbone controls scale but may disadvantage prompting baselines that depend on stronger reasoning or memory management. The paper does not quantify intent-extraction errors, leakage-audit agreement, or robustness across extractors.

## Reproduction and application

At commit `d37e3c062d30950f2a3744c66a18fda9aa72c1f7`, the repository contains 535 held-out sessions, 534 visitors, 8,957 item IDs, and 7,947 action-bearing steps. Static compilation passes for the environment, evaluator, and OPTD modules. The evaluation CLI loads, a reset/end environment smoke test passes, and one sampled remote interface image returns HTTP 200.

There is no automated test directory. The full 64,729-session dataset, training checkpoints, primary traces, result files, and end-to-end distributed training runner are not public. The artifact supports inspection of data structures, environment transitions, and metric implementations, but not independent reproduction of the primary table or production A/B comparison.

## Related work

- OPeRA provides real visual shopping traces and interactive evaluation but is primarily single-session; UserArena adds cross-session trajectories and production A/B validation.
- OmniBehavior provides longitudinal behavior records but has a less complete action space and visual context.
- A/B Agent studies recommendation policies with synthetic trajectories; UserArena uses real logs and production comparison.
- Customer-R1 applies reinforcement learning to shopping-user simulation and is a main text baseline for OPTD.
- On-Policy Distillation trains on student-generated responses; OPTD adds trajectory-level intent and a per-token trust region.

## Research judgment

- Priority: high for work on agent evaluation, user simulation, recommendation world models, long-horizon behavior, and offline policy validation.
- Confidence: medium-high. Real trajectories, a controlled backbone, layered metrics, ablations, and production comparison provide meaningful evidence, while statistical repetition, cross-platform validity, and complete artifacts remain weak.
- Watch next: full data and model release, multiple production policy comparisons, confidence intervals and multi-seed results, intent-extraction audits, and cross-market or cross-language validation.
