## Conclusion summary

1. The report measures GPT-6-Astra on 12 simulation benchmarks and 8 real machine configurations, organizes training demonstrations into frozen knowledge packages, and compares standard 1× and up to 10× action budgets.
2. High scores are highly dependent on information and budget conditions: RoboDojo improved from 27.62% at 1× to 81.90% at 10× in 210 episodes; this is not the same caliber victory as the standard budget baseline.
3. Harness ablation shows that System 2 auditing System 1 actions, semantic action interfaces, dynamic chunks and historical compression can all change the results; the highest success rate of 54.17% requires about 19.33M tokens/episode.
4. Real machine evidence directly exposes the reality gap: rigid grasping and dual-arm tasks can be successful, and the plug insertion of the three types of platforms totals 0/11; execution time, hardware protection, irreversible failure and observation obsolescence cannot be simply solved with 10× steps.

## Research question and background

Embodied benchmarks often mix model, harness, demo data, action interface, and step budget into one score. This report attempts to answer two things: where is the upper limit of a general model's capabilities when allowing knowledge bundles to be constructed from training demonstrations and relaxed operational budgets, and what is the gap between this upper limit and deployable embodied intelligence.

## Method

**Unified protocol:** Each task is first read by GPT-6-Astra and allowed to be used in training demonstrations, generating a knowledge package containing task guidance, motion and visual references, executable helpers, completion checks, recovery strategies and metadata. Knowledge packages are frozen before testing; each test episode uses a new session, and no information is shared between episodes.

**Budget experiment:** The same episode continues to run from 1× to a maximum of 10× action steps, retaining the environmental changes that have occurred; it stops on success, termination failure, or budget exhaustion. 10× refers to the upper limit of action steps, not the upper limit of token or wall clock time.

**Harness and real machines:** RoboDojo ablation changes planning, execution, feedback, history compression, action representation, chunk size and System 1/System 2 collaboration methods respectively. The real machine part covers single-arm, double-arm, dexterous hands, mobile manipulators and humanoid robots. Demonstrations are not used, but artificially structured contexts such as tool schemas, joint limits, and safety constraints are provided.

## Experiments and evidence

**Coverage:** 12 benchmarks including LIBERO series, RoboDojo, RoboLab, Bench2Dex, DexJoCo, SIMPLE, BEHAVIOR, VLN-CE and LHPR-VLN. Standard and extended budgets are reported separately, and 10× results should not be confused with the standard baseline.

**Representative results:** Under 10× conditions, LIBERO-Long++, LIBERO-Pro, LIBERO-Ultra-Long and SIMPLE reached 100.0%, 99.3%, 98.9%, 98.8% respectively; RoboDojo was 81.9%, BEHAVIOR was 37.0% under unexpanded budget, LHPR-VLN is still only 28.9%.

**ICL and cost:** SIMPLE's four common tasks increased from 70.8% with zero samples to 90.8% ICL in 120 episodes, but the average test token per successful episode increased from 2.64M to 6.40M, and single task preparation required another 8.34M tokens and 90.4 minutes. Each episode of LIBERO-Ultra-Long is about 9M tokens and nearly 2 hours; the RoboDojo expansion budget is more than 3 hours on average.

Mobile Search was 9/23; Unitree G1 Toy Grab and Drop was 2/3, and Steer, Approach and Stop was 3/3. The sample size is small, and some tasks use manually written crawl scripts or task-level prompts.

## Limitations and risks

**Explicit restrictions from the author:** Most of the knowledge packages are task-specific, and the migration becomes weak after leaving the demonstration coverage; the cost of tokens and wall clocks is high; the model will misjudge visually reasonable states as physical successes, and it is difficult to recover irreversible errors such as dumping and falling, and will also lose previous progress in long-term tasks.

**Research inference:** The action interfaces, demonstration coverage, success criteria and budgets of the 12 benchmarks are not unified. Cross-benchmark aggregation is more suitable for describing the capability map and is not suitable for establishing a single ranking. The 10× action budget, paused simulation, and ~98% cached input tokens together form a special cost structure that cannot be directly extrapolated to real robots. Most tasks in real-machine experiments have very few trials, and the quality of artificial context is also an uncontrolled variable.

## Reproduction and application

The official repository has fixed commit `10457ab4ebc0f14ffcd6c58cc96db6c6feb51b3f`, Apache-2.0; the universal runtime, LIBERO series adaptation, evaluation scripts and offline testing have been disclosed, and many adaptations such as RoboDojo and RoboLab are still marked Coming soon. The Python compilation of this site passed; during the collection phase, the unit test generated 36 errors due to the lack of dependencies such as Pillow, NumPy, pydantic-ai, claude-agent-sdk, openai-codex, etc. The unit test did not enter the test execution, and the business model and robot results were not reproduced.

The most feasible conclusion in engineering is not "the model has solved embodied intelligence", but: the action interface should be aligned with the model semantic reasoning; the frequency of System 2 review needs to be adjusted according to cost; completion determination, long-term status and irreversible failure should become the first-class mechanism of Harness.
