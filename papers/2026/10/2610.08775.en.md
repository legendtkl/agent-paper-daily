## Conclusion summary

1. The paper proposes "bottling": When faced with millions of similar instances, the Agent does not call expensive models one by one. Instead, it independently trains small models, writes programs, or combines reusable artifacts within a fixed time, computing power, and API budget.
2. BOTTLED measured this capability using 3 million workloads, 10 models, and unified OpenCode Harness. Each model-task is run 2 times, for a total of 60 main runs; each time is up to 10 hours, 1 A100 40GB, 8 CPU, 64GB RAM and weighted 5 million tokens.
3. General task knowledge is not equal to encapsulation ability: 48/60 times bottling is lower than the lower bound of the corresponding zero-shot 95% confidence interval, 31/60 times is inferior to the naive small model distillation baseline with the same token budget, and 21/60 times exhausts the budget.
4. The upper limit of capabilities is still considerable. Opus 5 retains about 82% of the zero-shot macro-F1 on ESCI and reports about 657 times lower cost; however, this success only occurs on some models and tasks and cannot be generalized to general cost reduction.
5. High priority for reading. The paper separately measures the completion of Agent's one-time tasks and "converting capabilities into repeatable, low-margin-cost workpieces." However, there are currently only 3 types of classification tasks and 2 Agent runs per grid. The public warehouse also lacks pollution audit and Jev evaluation code.

## Research question and background

Rather than solving a puzzle, much production work involves repeatedly extracting, classifying, or detecting millions of similar inputs. The cost of calling a cutting-edge model item by item increases approximately linearly, while the task requires only a fraction of the capabilities of the general model. Traditional agent benchmarks, which typically require the delivery of a single answer, code, or research result, do not measure whether the agent knows when it is worth investing upfront resources, what artifacts to construct, and how to trade off quality versus amortized cost.

Accordingly, the paper defines "encapsulation capability" as: the agent can independently choose to train small models, write rules or programs, continue to call APIs, and finally submit predictions for all instances on a complete workload without visible labels; the construction cost and execution cost are both subject to budget constraints.

## Method

**Full workload.** Agent initially gets all unlabeled input and can take advantage of the cross-instance structure. MAVE contains approximately 4.77 million item-attribute pairs, ESCI is a query-item correlation classification, and RAID contains approximately 5.62 million human or model texts. The indicators are F1, macro-F1 and AUROC respectively.

**Unified resource constraints.** All models are run in OpenCode for up to 10 hours at a time; the weighted token budget is 5 million, with output tokens counting 2x and cached inputs counting 0.1x. Agent can only call its own model API, and the network is recorded by the agent and blocks the source of data tags; missing outputs will be filled with uninformative default values ​​instead of ignored.

**Contrast design.** Zero-shot compares uniformly drawing 1,000 samples per task to estimate the cost of running the complete data one by one. For the naive distillation comparison, GLM 5.3 Flash was used to generate labels within the same budget of 5 million tokens, and then Qwen3-0.6B and SmolLM2-360M-Instruct were fine-tuned respectively without task-specific parameter adjustment. Aggregate scores are normalized by the task's uninformative baseline and theoretical upper limit.

**Differences from similar jobs.** MLE-bench, PaperBench and InferenceBench evaluate whether the Agent can complete complex research or optimization tasks; Programmatic Skill Learning and SkillWeaver learn reusable skills. The core difference of BOTTLED is that it directly delivers full batch workloads and puts front-end build investment, batch execution quality, and amortized costs into the same budget.

## Experiments and evidence

**Scale and repetition.** 10 models × 3 tasks × 2 independent Agent runs form 60 main experiments. The zero-shot score was bootstrapped 2,000 times on 1,000 uniform samples; the distilled baseline was run 3 times per task and student model. There are only 2 times per cell on the agent side, so the running variance estimate of the model ranking is still limited.

**Ability separation.** On MAVE, the zero-shot F1 of GPT 5.6 Terra and Sonnet 5 are both about 0.44, but the bottled F1 is 0.405 and 0.154 respectively. Overall, 48/60 runs were below the lower bound of the corresponding zero-shot 95% confidence interval, indicating that direct answer ability does not reliably predict the ability to construct artifacts.

**Naive baseline.** 31/60 times bottling is not as good as the stronger distillation results in the two small models. This comparison uses the same token budget and unified scripts, showing that handing the budget to the autonomous agent does not automatically outperform the fixed label generation and fine-tuning process.

**Cost frontier.** On ESCI, Opus 5 has an average bottled macro-F1 of 0.499 and a zero-shot of 0.609; the reported cost is $26.28 and the zero-shot estimate for the full workload is $17,273, about 657x lower. When compared to Jev, the Opus 5 achieves about 94% of its macro-F1 and costs about a quarter of the estimated full running cost of Jev; Jev is only available with ESCI and RAID, and the review code has not yet been made public.

**Budget failed.** 21/60 main runs were stopped by Harness due to exhaustion of tokens, 8 of which were not fully submitted. After canceling the hard termination and rerunning, there are still 47/60 times below the lower bound of the zero-shot confidence interval; another Agent prematurely announced completion when there were about 4.95 million tokens left, indicating that the problem is not only due to tight budgets.

**Local artifact inspection.** Official warehouse fixed commit `5a810d4be1e27333d04c51fa5870d4fcc8ce7120` public data preparation, budget MCP, agent rules, task prompts, Agent/zero-shot/distillation entry and configuration; all Python files are compiled and passed. Millions of pieces of data have not been downloaded, paid models have been called, or training has been rerun. The warehouse is currently clearly marked with contaminated judge and Jev evaluation codes that need to be filled.

## Limitations and risks

**Explicit restrictions by the author.** The complete zero-shot cost is extrapolated from 1,000 samples and does not actually traverse the entire workload; network blocking relies on URL blocklist, which may miss the source of label leakage, so the author uses an unpublished Agent judge to audit pollution. The experiment only covers 3 classification tasks and cannot represent generation, interaction or high-risk operation artifacts.

**Research inference.** There are only 2 Agent runs per model-task, and the Agent chooses its own solution, so run variance and accidental implementation choices can significantly affect rankings. Different models have different token prices, caching policies, and inference token pricing. Weighted token budgets control the form of resources but do not equate to the same dollars, energy consumption, or latency.**Compare boundaries.** Comparison with Jev only covers 2 tasks, and Jev costs are also projected to the complete data by sampling; "657 times lower cost" is an amortization result under the reported configuration and should not be extrapolated to a production environment with small batches or continuous task drift. Artifact maintenance, monitoring, retraining, and quality regression costs do not enter the main metric.

## Reproduction and application

Code is licensed under MIT license and comes with 3 data preparation scripts, OpenCode Harness, bubblewrap sandbox, OpenRouter configuration, budget and price list. Complete reproduction requires downloading public data, configuring Conda, A100-class GPUs, OpenRouter credentials, and incurring the cost of 60 Agent runs and distillation training for up to 10 hours.

In engineering, BOTTLED can be used as a pre-evaluation for batch automation: fix the input distribution and quality threshold, provide a development budget for the Agent, and let it deliver models, programs or mixed artifacts; then compare the total cost with manually written rules, fixed distillation processes and calling the model one by one. Amortized benefits only make sense if the batch size is large enough, the drift is slow, and the regression measurements are consistently valid.

## Related work

- **MLE-bench (arXiv:2410.07095):** Evaluates an Agent's ability to complete a machine learning engineering competition; BOTTLED focuses on constructing low marginal cost artifacts for complete repetitive workloads.
- **MLAgentBench (arXiv:2310.03302):** Evaluate the machine learning experimental capabilities of language agents; BOTTLED uses model training as an optional strategy rather than a preset task type.
- **Programmatic Skill Learning (arXiv:2608.11338):**  Reduce Agent costs through programmatic skills; BOTTLED provides a more general quality-amortized cost evaluation without limiting the shape of the workpiece.
- **InferenceBench (arXiv:2607.20468):** Evaluate Agent-optimized open LLM inference system; BOTTLED is oriented to specific batch tasks and takes the complete workload as the evaluation object.
- **OS-Marathon (arXiv:2601.20650):** Focus on ultra-long-range, repetitive Computer Use; BOTTLED allows the Agent to convert repetitiveness into offline artifacts instead of completing interface operations one after another.
