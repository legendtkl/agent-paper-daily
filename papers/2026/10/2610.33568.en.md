## Conclusion summary

- The paper replaces “collect successful traces” with two testable goals: cover underrepresented observation–action dependencies and supervise only decisions grounded in agent-visible history.
- Across five Qwen3 scales, the equal-weight mean over Tau1, Tau2, and BFCL rises by 16.9–29.8 points; the 8B Tau2 score increases from 25.00 to 74.17.
- Gains concentrate in stateful and multi-turn interaction. BFCL Live Simple falls by 6–13 points at four of five scales, so the method is not uniformly beneficial.
- The authors release code, seven shards representing 34,772 semantic interactions, and five checkpoints. All 85 repository tests passed locally, but raw generation logs, task instances, full result traces, and run receipts are absent.

## Research question and background

Terminal success is an inadequate filter for tool-agent supervision: a successful trajectory can be shallow or contain actions unsupported by the learner-visible context. Among 34,772 candidate semantic interactions, 40.9% contain at most two supervised decisions and only 10.0% include a decision after a real tool error. The paper therefore asks how to construct interactions whose dependencies are represented and whose actions are learnable.

## Method

Trajectory2Gym maps tool interactions into semantic structures with observation, action, and dependency edges, then identifies under-supported compositions. It expands executable tasks around those structures, executes them, and retains only decisions justified by the visible history. Structural-support selection allocates examples, while parent-normalized SFT makes one semantic interaction—not its token count—the unit of training weight.

Unlike success-only filtering, terminal success is merely an admission condition. Structure coverage and decision grounding select supervision. Relative to ToolACE, MidTool, and EnvFactory-style synthesis, the contribution is primarily what interactions to create and how to weight them, rather than adding more schemas or tasks.

## Experiments and evidence

- Qwen3 1.7B, 4B, 8B, 14B, and 30B-A3B receive one epoch of full-parameter SFT on roughly 11.6k–19.9k trajectories per scale; each task is run once.
- Tau2 rises from 19.17 to 69.17 at 4B, 25.00 to 74.17 at 8B, and 28.33 to 84.17 at 30B-A3B. Twenty-four of 25 domain-by-scale pairs improve.
- BFCL overall improves at every scale, driven by multi-turn subsets; Live Simple regression shows a distributional trade-off on easy calls.
- At 8B, targeted expansion, decision grounding, structural selection, and parent normalization contribute 3.55, 2.75, 2.32, and 8.04 mean-score points in separate ablations. Single runs and interacting components prevent additive causal interpretation.
- Support depth two scores 72.28, above depth three at 70.55 and depth four at 69.44. More and deeper synthetic data is not monotonically better.
- Tau1 Airline is a seen-task, transductive setting. Closed models and external methods are not matched for compute, data, or training protocol.

## Limitations and risks

The authors explicitly leave web, software-engineering, terminal, and online training settings for future work. Main-table tasks are run once with no variance or confidence intervals. Evaluation on Tau/BFCL can entangle structural design with benchmark adaptation, the simple-call regression may be a material deployment trade-off, and the weighting comparison is not fully controlled for tokens and compute.

## Reproduction and application

The reviewed repository commit is `da4fa739e69ec8d2da64cf76a2747ec06036bab9`, licensed Apache-2.0. The dataset has 22 files; seven compressed JSONL shards total about 125 MB. Five public model repositories are downloadable. On 2026-10-28, all 85 tests passed with Python 3.12 and PyTorch 2.7.1.

The release supports inspection of schemas, supervision masks, normalization, and training views. It does not include raw generation requests, task instances, full experimental trajectories, configuration snapshots, or run receipts, and users must reconstruct Tau/BFCL environments. The work is actionable for tool-agent SFT and trajectory governance but should not be extrapolated directly to web or coding agents.

## Related work

- [Tau-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045): stateful tool-agent evaluation used by this paper.
- [Tau2-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07982): two-sided multi-turn interaction, where the strongest gains appear.
- [BFCL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04249): function-calling evaluation; Live Simple provides an important counterexample.
- [ToolACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00920): large-scale synthetic tool-learning data; Trajectory2Gym adds structural and grounding constraints.
- [FireAct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05915): multi-trajectory agent fine-tuning; this paper focuses on which internal decisions are valid supervision.

## Research judgment

- Reading priority: high. It turns tool-trajectory success into two mechanism-level questions with targeted ablations.
- Confidence: medium-high. Directional consistency spans five scales and three benchmarks, with public code, data, and weights; single runs, same-domain evaluation, and missing receipts limit causal strength.
- Audience: tool-agent training, trajectory synthesis, SFT data governance, and evaluation teams.
- Watch next: independent reruns, equal-token/equal-compute controls, Live Simple regression analysis, and transfer to web, terminal, and coding environments.
