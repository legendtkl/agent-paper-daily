## Conclusion summary

- MCP-Injector perturbs the MCP tool-call boundary with content errors, explicit errors, delay, and silent drops under permanent, transient, and intermittent schedules.
- Its profile comes from 57,240 English issue/PR threads across 1,774 public MCP repositories. It measures the composition of reported faults, not production incidence, and its 91.3% permanent share makes it a stress profile.
- The 1,350-cell experiment crosses two model sizes, three agent stacks, three proxy conditions, 25 tasks, and three seeds. Faults reduce task-completion score from 8.08 to 3.23/10, a 60% drop.
- “Scale does not purchase fault tolerance” is bounded to 20B and 120B variants from one family and one serving setup. The resilience-ratio test has `p=0.96`, and 91.1% versus 95.6% completion does not establish that the larger model is worse.
- Released artifacts include a 1,350-entry manifest, fault logs, and analysis mappings. Core modules compile statically; full reproduction requires local models, APIs, and roughly 58 hours of agent execution.

## Research question and background

Tool-agent benchmarks usually assume tools return correct results on time, so they cannot measure degradation under malformed values, semantic errors, delays, silent drops, or persistent faults. Infrastructure chaos engineering does not express MCP-semantic failures. This work separates model capability from tool-boundary resilience through reproducible, weighted fault injection.

## Method

The middleware runs Observe, Decide, Act, and Record at `call_tool`. OP0 passes through; OP1/OP2 corrupt content; OP3 changes types; OP4 returns explicit errors; OP5 delays; OP6 drops calls. Rules specify scope, selection weight, and persistence, with seeded schedules and structured logs.

The profile starts from 103,166 consolidated threads across 1,774 active repositories and retains 57,240 after deduplication and language/build filtering. GPT-4o classifies them under a fixed JSON schema. Two senior researchers independently annotate a stratified 117-thread sample with adjudication: Cohen's κ is 0.93 for mode and 0.80 for persistence. Eighteen mode-by-persistence weights come from 13,824 labeled faults.

## Experiments and evidence

The study uses 25 single-server MCP-Bench tasks without distraction servers. Two MXFP4 gpt-oss models run with the same 16K context; the stacks are MCP-Bench, OpenAI Agents SDK, and LangChain. Proxy-off, OP0, and OPP each use three seeds, yielding 1,350 cells. GPT-4o-mini scores each completed run five times with randomized rubric order and median aggregation.

Across 450 OPP cells paired with OP0, task completion drops 60%, tool selection 59%, planning effectiveness 67%, and six other metrics 54–68%. Absolute losses are 4.4–5.2/10 with paired 95% intervals around ±0.4 and BH-adjusted Wilcoxon `p<0.001`. Runs completed in both conditions still lose 57%; 56% of OPP cells show partial degradation rather than outright failure.

No proxy-off versus OP0 comparison is significant after 18 Bonferroni corrections; estimated overhead is 0.1–0.5 seconds per tool call. The released manifest reports 1,350 records, 450 per stack, and 75 per stack-model-proxy combination; the fault log contains 1,905 events. This validates artifact structure, not an independent rerun.

## Limitations and risks

The authors note judge noise and rubric credit for some ungrounded answers, issue-reporting bias toward persistent failures, and missing operators for schema drift, truncation, and partial omission. Both models are from one family; fault-free MCP-Bench is easy; the 25 tasks are single-server.

Research judgment: stack differences jointly change error handling and call volume—8.6 mean fault events for MCP-Bench, 2.3 for LangChain, and 1.8 for OpenAI—so exposure and recovery remain partly entangled. Only nine of 1,905 events are transient or intermittent. The middleware is dual-use and should remain in authorized pre-deployment environments.

## Reproduction and application

GitLab and Zenodo release Apache-2.0 code, pinned dependencies, tasks, profiles, five campaign rounds, formatted results, notebooks, figure/table mappings, and data licenses. Six Python files under `mcp_modules` compile successfully in this review.

Full reproduction needs gpt-oss-20B/120B, LM Studio, an OpenAI judge, and five classes of external API. The final campaign reportedly consumes about 58 agent-execution hours plus 18 judge-API hours, so it was not rerun. The artifact supports pre-deployment resilience comparisons, not production failure-rate estimation or adversarial security certification.

## Research judgment

- Priority: high for MCP gateways, tool-agent reliability, harness evaluation, and pre-deployment testing.
- Confidence: higher than typical small-sample resilience work because of paired design, corrections, sensitivity analyses, and cell-level artifacts; generalization remains narrow.
