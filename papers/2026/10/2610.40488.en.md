- Of 415 registered MVEs, 412 are delivered, 369 are model-audited as sound, and 194 as sound/valuable/new; 73 are also judged beyond the seed paper and non-obvious.
- The agent's first choice is the auditors' most valued MVE in 38% of conditions versus 20% for random choice, but only 3 points above the first-written MVE, with a 95% CI of -8 to 15. The ranking has no demonstrated incremental value.

## Research question and background

Research agents can read papers, write code, and run experiments, but execution ability does not show that they know what deserves to run first. Obvious or likely-to-succeed experiments can waste a limited budget even when executed correctly. rTaste therefore tests whether proposal, pre-outcome forecast, priority, and eventual research value align.

## Method

Six frontier models each work as a single researcher and as a multi-agent team on seven recent NLP papers and their code. Every condition registers five MVEs with a hypothesis, closest work, executable plan, threshold event, cost, and reproduction conditions, then forecasts event probabilities and ranks the five.

Independent executors implement every MVE under equal budgets, without using the ranking. A rerun on a fresh machine decides event occurrence. Two model judges search novelty; three auditors assess fidelity, validity, value, and ranking; three reviewers classify the relation to the seed paper, obviousness, and evidential meaning.

## Experiments and evidence

- 83/84 conditions produce MVEs. Of 415 registered MVEs, 412 are delivered, 369 receive majority-sound labels, and 194 are sound/valuable/new.
- 66% build on the seed paper, 24% check it, and only 5% open a new direction. Forty-four percent match obvious follow-ups listed directly from the paper.
- Agent Brier score is 0.192; the same-model judge using only the paper and registrations scores 0.198. The difference is -0.006 with a 95% CI of -0.021 to 0.009, showing no measurable gain from the agent's extra search and code access.
- The agent, first-written MVE, and same-model judge select the auditors' favorite in 38%, 34%, and 36% of conditions. Agent minus first-written is 3 points, 95% CI -8 to 15.
- Teams add 0.71 sound/valuable/new MVEs per five, 95% CI -0.10 to 1.52. Mean cost rises from $15.31 to $23.93, and yield per $100 falls from 13 to 11.
- Auditor agreement is uneven: pairwise Cohen's kappa is 0.09–0.32 for faithfulness and 0.56–0.69 for research value; novelty-judge kappa is 0.27.

## Limitations and risks

All quality, novelty, and value labels come from models, with no human baseline. Two auditors are also evaluated models and executors. Every MVE runs regardless of rank, so ranking never controls a real budget. Seven seed papers provide a small cluster count for uncertainty estimates.

Further concerns are sensitivity to the chosen value threshold, majority rule, and strict novelty conjunction; potential hindsight bias because reviewers see outcomes when judging non-obviousness; and an unbalanced Claude Opus 5.5 single-researcher condition that completes only six papers.

In practice, research-agent systems should separately record proposal, forecast, ranking, and execution, freezing thresholds before results. Validity, informativeness, novelty, and value should remain distinct from whether an experiment merely succeeded.

## Research judgment

- Reading priority: high. The study uses falsifiable negative results to constrain broad claims that research agents already possess taste.
- Confidence: medium-high. Pre-registration, exhaustive execution, reruns, and public data are strong; value, novelty, and priority ground truth remain model-generated.
- Relevant to: AI scientists, research agents, experiment orchestration, evaluation, and research-budget allocation.
- Watch next: human baselines, more domains, real fixed-budget adaptive selection, label-threshold sensitivity, and whether rankings matter when only a few experiments can run.
