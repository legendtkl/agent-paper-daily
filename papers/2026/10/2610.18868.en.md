## Conclusion summary

- The paper splits the sparse reward problem of long-range Agent training into two objects: first, reversely generate the task from the hidden correct world, and then use the difference between the intermediate workspace and the world to calculate the verifiable progress.
- Tev-Gym contains 1,170 environments, 7 realms; environment generation calls no models, median CPU time for single environment generation is 30 seconds, and passes 8-category eligibility gate checks for bonus causality, vulnerability, role independence, and cross-seed differentiation.
- On ALE-CM, Tev-27B's Pass@5 improved from 0.17 for base to 0.28; on the Computing and Mathematics subset of Terminal-Bench 3.0/4.0, Pass@1 improved from 0.09/0.04 to 0.14/0.10.
- Evidence supports the mechanism that "intensive progress rewards can alleviate the failure of all-failure rollout groups without gradients." However, external migration only covers 18 to 22 tasks. The code and environment generator have not yet been released, and key conclusions cannot be independently rerun.

## Research question and background

The final pass rate of long-distance tasks is low. GRPO will get an all-zero intra-group advantage when a group of rollouts for the same task all fail, and the gradient is most lacking in the early stages of training. Existing terminal environments mostly use unit tests or final state pass/failure as rewards. It is difficult to tell how far an unfinished rollout has progressed, and it is also difficult to distinguish real progress from local speculation.

The key issue of the paper is not to simply expand the number of tasks, but to construct a data source that meets three conditions at the same time: the correct final state can be guaranteed by the generation process, the intermediate progress can be verified according to the workspace status, and the task difficulty is enough to cover minute to hour-level processes.

## Method

**Latent-world-first synthesis.** Each generator determines the hidden world, workflow DAG, correct final state, and corruption plan before rendering the Agent visible files and injecting missing, wrong, or inconsistent states. The strict verifier checks the final state, and the progress function scores the workspace by DAG nodes and their preconditions. The paper reports that each environment needs to pass eight types of gate control: reward causality, exploit resistance, role independence, solver agreement, progress validity, metamorphic invariance, six-seed diversity and public-contract hygiene.

**PPRS.** The reward at step t uses the progress potential energy difference Φ(s_t)−Φ(s\_{t−1}), and the total rollout reward is the indicated value plus 0.25×Φ(s_T). This form retains the final progress item, so it is not the traditional potential-based shaping where the strategy remains unchanged; the author intentionally changes the training objectives so that the all-failure group can still produce a relative advantage according to the final progress. The reward for passing a rollout is at least 1, while the progress item for a failed rollout does not exceed 0.25, and passing priority is still maintained at the single track level.

**Training process.** Tev-9B uses 264 unit-tier tasks; Tev-27B uses all 1,170 environments. Each step extracts 8 tasks, 24 rollouts per task, a maximum of 65,536 response tokens, and 64 tool calls. 27B training requires approximately 4 B200 nodes or 8 A100 nodes to run for 4 days.

**Differences from similar jobs.** Compared with SWE-Smith, TMax, SETA and Endless Terminals, the main difference of Tev-Gym is not the scale, but the simultaneous export of final state verifier and staged progress function from the same hidden world; the median Agent execution time of each environment is 12.4 minutes, and the Pass@1 of Gemini 3.6 Flash is 29%, which is obviously more difficult than the short-range environment in the table.

## Experiments and evidence

**ALE-CM.** This subset contains 18 computational and mathematical tasks that can be run under the current execution conditions, 5 times per question per model. The average Tev-27B score increased from 0.33 to 0.40 for S@1, from 0.12 to 0.16 for Pass@1, and from 0.17 to 0.28 for Pass@5. "Matching GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5" in the abstract only means that the Pass@5 on the 18-question subset is both 0.28, and does not mean that the complete ALE or the average score are the same.

**External migration.** On the 22-question set of Terminal-Bench 3.0, Tev-27B Pass@1 improved from 0.09 to 0.14; on the 18-question set of Terminal-Bench 4.0, it improved from 0.04 to 0.10. Compared with GLM-5.3-Flash, Tev-320B improves from 0.20/0.29 to 0.27/0.37 on two subsets respectively. On question 89 of TB 2.1, Tev-27B improved from 0.764 to 0.794, indicating that training did not significantly damage the mid-range terminal capability.

**Ablation. In the **9B setting, the S@1/Pass@5 of the complete PPRS is 0.29/0.11, and the final state sparse reward is 0.25/0.06; the public-view verifier and prompt-first data structures are only 0.24/0.06 and 0.23/0.06 respectively. The author also claims that 27B sparse rewards are almost all zero-advantage groups and cannot complete an optimization step, but this result is not supported by independent tables or curves.

**Contamination check.** The authors checked 8-grams, 13-grams, and prompt Jaccard and found no long n-gram overlap between the Tev-Gym environment and the four assessment sets; this reduces the risk of direct text leakage, but does not rule out indirect overlap in task mechanics or domain structure.

## Limitations and risks

**Explicit restrictions by the author.** Current research focuses on synthetic terminal environments; still significantly behind cutting-edge Agents on the most difficult benchmarks; scaling new workflows requires continued writing and verification of generator components.

**Research inference.** The external transfer subset only has 18 to 22 questions, and 0.04 to 0.10 roughly corresponds to solving one more task on 18 questions. The paper does not give a confidence interval. The guarantee that the hidden world can be "uniquely restored from the initial state" is also stronger than the actual access control: corruption such as deleting records and blanking fields is not naturally reversible. PPRS retains the final potential energy and may prefer the "nearly completed but failed" strategy. The paper only gives theoretical boundaries and does not report the complete non-submission rate.

**Recurrence risk.** As of the time of collection, the project page still marks Code as "soon", and no generator, environment, training script or fixed commit has been released. Two models have been released, but both are CC BY-NC 4.0 and have low downloads; the model weights are not sufficient to double-check task generation, reward correctness, and training ablation.

## Reproduction and applicationFull training requires about 4 days with a long context rollout, a resumable sandbox, and 4 B200 or 8 A100 nodes. Currently, the only artifacts that can be inspected are papers, project pages, and Tev-27B/320B model cards. The 1,170 environments and PPRS training described in the paper cannot be performed. There is no independent rerun model or benchmark in this round.

What is most worthy of reuse in engineering is not the specific model, but the environmental contract that has the same origin as "hiding the correct world - stage DAG - strict final state - progress potential energy". It is suitable for processes with checkable intermediate states such as data cleaning, infrastructure recovery, and scientific computing; it is not suitable for open writing, subjective review, or tasks where a unique intermediate truth value cannot be defined.

## Related work

- [SETA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10891): Expand the scale of the terminal Agent environment, but mainly use final state or test proportional signals.
- [TMax](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23321): Provides large-scale terminal tasks and training recipes, with task duration and reward granularity lower than Tev.
- [Endless Terminals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16443): Programmatically expand the terminal environment, focusing on the number of environments and sustainable generation.
- [T1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11042): Reinforcement learning for long-term terminal tasks still relies on verifiable final states.
- [ALE-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09050): Provides long-range goal-driven algorithm engineering tasks and is an important reference in the field of Tev training and ALE-CM evaluation.

## Research judgment

**Reading Priority:** High. Ideal for agent training, executable environment and verifier designers.

**Confidence:** Medium to high. The core mechanism, data structure and 9B ablation are relatively complete; the statistical stability and code reproducibility of external migration are still insufficient.

**Follow-up observations:** Wait for the generator, environment and training code to be released; review the 27B sparse reward control experiment; report confidence intervals on the full Terminal-Bench with more random seeds; check whether different corruption types can indeed uniquely determine the hidden world from the Agent's visible state.
