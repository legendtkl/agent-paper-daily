## Conclusion summary

- The paper models Agent context as a produce→deliver→act→retain→transfer→persist pipeline. Its core question is not window size but whether tool evidence reaches the model intact.
- Across 720 runs, 3,827 of 11,189 tool results were prefix-truncated at a fixed character count, and every truncated result became invalid JSON. Two adjacent fields in the same result had delivery rates of 4.7% and 99.3%.
- The registered controlled experiment does not show lower spawned-fault accuracy, but it does show compensation cost: median tool calls rise from 53 to 68/76, computations from 18 to 32/36, and yield falls from 0.61 to 0.34/0.31.
- A Cairn Commons standard-library probe independently reaggregates the public artifact's mechanical truncation result. It does not run a model or settle the causal accuracy question for a quiet culprit.

## Research question and background

Agent research often describes context using window size and final-answer accuracy, while tool outputs can already have been lost during serialization, truncation, cross-turn retention, worker handoff, or cross-run persistence. Window capacity says what a model could read, not what the harness actually delivered. The paper therefore asks how much evidence is lost at each boundary, whether that changes answers or work, and whether common frameworks implement similar mechanisms.

## Method

The paper divides delivery into five boundaries. S1 covers tool production to model-visible delivery; S2 is the action step budget; S3 controls retained history; S4 controls whether worker findings reach the decision stage; and S5 controls cross-run persistence. Each boundary uses its own loss unit rather than collapsing all failures into accuracy.

The concrete S1 defect takes a fixed-length prefix of serialized JSON. Stable key order makes later fields disappear wholesale and can make the payload unparsable. The authors first measure field traces, replay 864 records through prefix and row-aware cutters, then reintroduce defects one at a time while holding the model, tools, task, and prompt fixed.

## Experiments and evidence

- In 720 field runs, 3,827 of 11,189 tool results were truncated, and all truncated results broke. One field reached the model in 4.7% of 742 calls and an adjacent field in 99.3%. The largest single prompt was about 23k tokens, 5.75% of a 400k window.
- The replay contains 864 results from 31 runs. Every over-budget result breaks under prefix slicing and none under row-aware cutting. A real 21-container trace produces 33,370 characters, 5.6 times the 6,000-character cap: 30 of 118 early records survive, while all 128 late records disappear.
- Each fault class has five distinct recordings and three repeats, for 145 runs. Table 2 reports spawned culprits: repaired, S1, and reversed-key S1 yield 14/15, 15/15, and 15/15 correct; median tool calls are 53, 68, and 76; computations are 18, 32, and 36; yield is 0.61, 0.34, and 0.31.
- The authors inspect seven framework versions. langchain-core prefix-slices, smolagents removes the middle, three do not cut tool outputs, one cuts only tracing data, and one cuts on whole lines. This establishes recurrence, not prevalence.

## Limitations and risks

The authors explicitly limit the end-to-end evidence to one harness, one kernel-trace diagnosis domain, and one model. Each class has five independent recordings with paired repeats. Field accuracy is confounded by analysis tools introduced with the delivery repair, and a causal accuracy experiment for quiet culprits remains future work.

Further concerns are that the contribution says all five stages have measured failures even though S5 is a disabled design choice; S3 is one before/after run; and the 98 empty S4 handoffs are not conditioned on workers having produced a transferable finding. Table 2 does not report all resident, S2–S4, or all-defects arms and gives no confidence intervals. Prefix and middle deletion create different field biases and should not be assigned one common risk rate.

## Reproduction and application

Zenodo hosts a 74,394,139-byte MIT-licensed package with the harness, drivers, scorer, derived results, transcript corpus, and registered plan. The multi-gigabyte raw LTTng/CTF captures are not included and are available only on request.

Cairn Commons independently checks 10,278 dictionary tool results in 161 packaged transcripts: all originals parse, while all 1,486 prefix slices above 6,000 characters are invalid JSON. The probe imports no author program, makes no inference call, and is not a complete reproduction of the paper's separate 864-record E1b cohort.

This run verified the Zenodo MD5 `424ffd1413480ae2d03024cb1316fa40` and executed the supplied scripts. `analyze.py` re-scored 145/145 cells, `replay_delivery.py` replayed 10,278 tool results and found 1,486 truncated/invalid-JSON outputs, `mechanism_delivery.py` reproduced the 6,000-character delivery cliff, and `test_paging.py` passed 6/6 tests. This confirms that the public package runs and recomputes its derived results; it is not an independent model experiment and does not rerun the unavailable raw-trace collection.

A minimal engineering adoption is to diff original tool output against model-visible output at the field level, forbid mid-JSON truncation, use record-aware cutting or paging, assert parseability in CI, and report field delivery, step-budget saturation, and worker-handoff rates.

## Related work

- [Lost in the Middle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172) studies how a model uses information already in its window; this paper asks whether the harness deleted it first.
- [RULER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06654) measures usable long-context capacity; usable capacity still does not prove complete delivery.
- [HarnessBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27922) compares outcome variance across harness designs; this paper localizes one variance source to a measurable delivery boundary.
- [An Empirical Study of Harness Design for Coding Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20804) compares context-management policies; this paper tracks which evidence each policy removes.
- [Same Simulation, Different Evidence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26836) studies accuracy costs of retrieved tool outputs; this paper covers destructive delivery before retrieval.

## Research judgment

- Reading priority: high. It identifies a harness defect that window utilization and final accuracy can hide but CI can catch.
- Confidence: medium-high. The mechanical truncation and invalid-JSON result has a public artifact and independent reaggregation; causal accuracy, cross-domain generality, and the full five-stage model need more evidence.
- Relevant to: Agent harness, tool protocol, context management, runtime observability, and Agent evaluation teams.
- Watch next: the registered quiet-culprit experiment, complete resident/all-defects results, replay through real framework cutters, whether field-level loss predicts harder tasks, and access to raw traces.
