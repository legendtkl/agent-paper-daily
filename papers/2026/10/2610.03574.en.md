## Conclusion summary

- HyperBrowseComp uses 423 human-validated questions to extend web-agent evaluation across 13 languages and heterogeneous evidence such as video, scanned PDFs, images, and maps; about 64.3% require non-text evidence.
- The best provider-native setup, Gemini 3.7 Flash, reaches only 31.68%; all five native-search models jointly fail 244/423 questions, indicating a shared evidence-discovery problem.
- A shared Exa retriever does not uniformly solve the task: GPT-5.6 Sol rises from 19.15% to 26.71% but consumes 928.8M tokens, making tools and budgets part of the ranking.
- Live-web drift, five evaluated frontier models, a 30-question human study in only three languages, and unavailable data/traces limit interpretation.

## Research question and background

Most browsing benchmarks emphasize English text pages and directly searchable answers. They under-measure persistent search across lower-resource languages, video timestamps, scanned documents, maps, and cross-page clue chains. HyperBrowseComp uses manually authored, native-speaker-validated short-answer questions whose evidence is publicly checkable.

## Method

The dataset contains 423 questions in 13 languages. Proficient speakers author questions and evidence, offline models filter questions answerable from parametric memory, and humans validate the remainder. Three harness modes are evaluated: provider-native search, shared Exa retrieval, and the OWL browsing agent, under a common short-answer protocol with saved evidence traces.

The benchmark deliberately targets rare, multimodal, multi-hop cases. It measures capability boundaries and failure structure, not average production-query success.

## Experiments and evidence

About 64.3% require non-text modalities: video 39.0%, PDF/book/OCR 29.8%, image 18.4%, and arithmetic 28.1%. Categories overlap.

| Setup | Accuracy | Total tokens | Total time |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Gemini 3.7 Flash/native | 31.68% | 14.1M | 129.5h |
| Gemini 3.1 Pro/native | 25.53% | 10.2M | 45.3h |
| GPT-5.6 Sol/native | 19.15% | 43.6M | 19.9h |
| GPT-5.6 Sol/Exa | 26.71% | 928.8M | 75.1h |
| Gemini/OWL | 17.73% | — | — |

OWL terminates on 93/423 questions; conditional accuracy on completed runs is 22.73%. The human study covers 30 Indonesian, Thai, and Vietnamese questions: humans solve 15, Gemini 13, with only eight overlapping. Human mean time is 89.5 minutes and median 54.6. This small slice cannot represent all languages and modalities.

## Limitations and risks

- The authors state that this intentionally difficult tail is not a natural query distribution; live pages may change, disappear, or vary by region.
- Only five frontier models are evaluated, while native tools, budgets, and stopping rules are not fully equivalent.
- The 30-question, three-language human comparison cannot establish a stable human ceiling.
- Exa token use is over an order of magnitude above native search in the reported GPT-5.6 Sol comparison, so accuracy must be paired with cost.
- Data and traces lacked a download endpoint at collection time, blocking independent answer and snapshot audits.

## Reproduction and application

Reproduction needs the fixed questions, answers, evidence, run-time web snapshots, three harnesses, pinned model versions, token/time budgets, and grader. The benchmark fits web-agent selection, multilingual retrieval, and multimodal routing. Production decisions also need a natural-traffic set and joint accuracy, failure-rate, latency, and token reporting.

## Related work

- [GAIA](https://openreview.net/forum?id=fibxvahvs3) evaluates real-world multi-step tool use; HyperBrowseComp emphasizes long-tail open-web languages and evidence modalities.
- [WebArena](https://openreview.net/forum?id=oKn9c6ytLx) uses reproducible websites; this paper trades reproducibility for the live open web.
- [MM-BrowseComp](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13186) adds multimodal browsing; HyperBrowseComp broadens languages and evidence types and records harness costs.
- BrowseComp targets hard-to-find short answers; this work generalizes the idea to 13 languages and heterogeneous media.
