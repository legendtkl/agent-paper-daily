## Conclusion summary

- ReTerm does not treat failure trajectories only as negative samples, but retains the environment state after the failure, allowing the Agent to continue diagnosis, repair and verification from the true residual state.
- ReTerm-10K uses basic model failure, strong teacher recovery and four-step filtering to construct approximately 10,000 recovery trajectories; ReTerm-GRPO then forks multiple recovery attempts from the failure state of the current policy, and forms a relative advantage within the group based on the verifier results.
- Qwen3.5-9B base is 20.2% on Terminal-Bench 2.0, standard GRPO is 28.0%, and ReTerm-GRPO is 34.8%; the same model has a gain of 6.7 to 14.6 percentage points on the four harnesses.
- The code, data and model have been made public, 391 Python files on the host side were compiled and passed, and the lightweight test 17 passed/1 skipped/1 failed; the failure was due to the lack of NumPy and typing_extensions on the local machine, not the failure of the algorithm assertion. Full training and evaluation still require 16 A100, VM snapshots, long context rollout and original task environment.

## Research question and background

Errors in the terminal agent will change the subsequent environment: the wrong command may leave behind processes, files, configurations or half-finished products. Static self-correction usually only changes the answer and cannot handle states contaminated by previous actions. Existing recovery data are often obtained by manually injecting errors, and may be inconsistent with the failure distribution actually generated by the model.

The paper therefore defines recovery as an independent training goal: continue execution from the environment and history after the Agent's own failure, and ultimately the original task verifier will still judge whether it is truly completed, rather than the language model judging "it seems to have been fixed."

## Method

**ReTerm-10K.** Basic Qwen3.5-9B is executed multiple times on the TMax-15K task. The system saves VM snapshots and complete interaction history before verifier runs, and only retains failure samples. DeepSeek-V4-Pro continues to recover from the original failed state without knowing the details of the hidden test. Candidate trajectories are filtered by verifier, format, constraint compliance and efficiency in sequence; the latter two are first marked by rules and then determined by DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash.

**Resumption of supervision.** SFT input includes the original task, failure history and short recovery prompts. The loss is only calculated by the teacher's recovery and continuation token, and the previous failed actions are not relearned. Of approximately 10,000 pieces of data, 6,000 were used for SFT and 4,000 task instances were used for subsequent RL.

**ReTerm-GRPO.** Each training step samples the original task group and the restored task group at the same time. After the original task rollout fails, the history and environment status are saved and written to the bounded queue; the subsequent rollout is independently resumed by forking G strips from the same failed state. The original task group and the recovery group calculate the intra-group advantages based on the verifier results, and then jointly update the same strategy. The queue uses new failures first and eliminates the oldest failures, reducing distribution dislocation caused by policy changes.

**Differences from similar jobs.** TermiGen synthesizes recovery trajectories by actively injecting errors, and ReTerm uses the actual failures of the current strategy instead; Reflexion and Agent-R mainly correct errors at the level of text reflection or iterative self-training, while ReTerm retains the executable environment state and uses the original task verifier as the recovery success criterion.

## Experiments and evidence

**Main results.**  Under the same lightweight ReAct harness, ReTerm-9B improved from 44.2%/20.2%/45.2% to 60.6%/34.8%/58.4% on OpenThoughts-TBLite, Terminal-Bench 2.0 and SWE-bench Verified respectively compared to Qwen3.5-9B. The paper uses its own paper or official published results for other models, so the absolute ranking across models cannot be regarded as a strict comparison of the same configuration.

**harness migration.**  Fixed Terminal-Bench 2.0 task, ReTerm-9B outperforms base by 14.6, 10.1, 6.7 and 12.4 percentage points on author harness, Terminus-2, mini-SWE-agent and OpenHands respectively. This result shows that the gain is not entirely dependent on the training time interface, but only Pass@1 is reported for each configuration, without repeated seeds or confidence intervals.

**Out-of-region migration.** AIME 2024/2025, Aider Polyglot, and SpreadsheetBench Verified increased by 10.0, 20.0, 3.1, and 11.0 percentage points respectively. The math tasks did not involve environmental reinstatement, so these gains may include general posttraining effects and cannot be entirely attributed to reinstatement mechanisms.

**Ablation and error analysis.** On Terminal-Bench 2.0, the base model, SFT, standard GRPO, and ReTerm-GRPO are 20.2%, 25.8%, 28.0%, and 34.8% respectively. The author uses DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash to classify five types of errors and review them manually; the recovery rate of ReTerm-GRPO in each category is higher than that of base and standard GRPO, but except for tool usage errors, the remaining categories still do not exceed 32%.

## Limitations and risks

**Limitations clearly shown by the author's evidence.** Most planning, implementation, verification, and resource management errors remain unrecoverable; the paper does not give full training costs, evaluation confidence intervals, or multiple random seed results. Inference fixes seed 0, weakening the judgment of sampling stability.

**Research inference.** The data relies on DeepSeek-V4-Pro teacher and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash filter, and may inherit teacher preferences and missed judgments; the recovery task comes from TMax-15K. Although there is external evaluation, the real production state contamination may still be more complicated. Gains on math and tabulation tasks cannot differentiate between the contributions of “recovery training” and general high-quality long-trajectory post-training.

**Licensing boundaries.** ReTerm-10K is marked ODC-BY, ReTerm-9B is marked Apache-2.0; the GitHub root directory does not provide a top-level project license, and only third-party dependencies are recorded in THIRD_PARTY.md. Before code reuse, you need to further confirm the author's permission for your own training code.

## Reproduction and applicationThe official release branch is released, and the fixed commit for this round is `7b97b2b7156c99175dc132c2f092ba0df873e7f5`. 391 non-vendored Python files via `py_compile`. Tests without training dependencies got 17 passed, 1 skipped, 1 failed; the failures come from the current Python 3.13 environment missing NumPy and typing_extensions. The remaining tests containing SFT/RL were not run during the collection phase due to missing transformers, torch and NumPy. In this round, the GPU training stack was not installed, and the paper benchmark was not rerun.

The paper training uses 16 A100 80GB images. Each step includes 8 original task groups and 2 recovery task groups, each with 16 rollouts; a single rollout can have up to 8,192 generated tokens, 65k contexts, and 32 interaction rounds. Applying this method requires the task environment to support execution, state saving/restoration and decidable verifier. It is suitable for terminal, warehouse repair and executable data tasks. It is not suitable for work that cannot reliably save side effects or has no objective final state.

## Related work

- [TermiGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07274): Synthetic recovery trajectories via active error injection, ReTerm uses the model's own failure status instead.
- [TMax](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23321): Mission source and important terminal training baseline for ReTerm-10K.
- [Agent-R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11425): Using iterative self-training to learn reflection, environment state retention is not as straightforward as ReTerm.
- [Reflexion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11366): Write failure feedback as text memory, which belongs to reflection during deployment rather than weight-level recovery training.
- [OpenThoughts-Agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24855): Provides terminal Agent data recipes and evaluation, which is the relevant training and benchmark background of ReTerm.

## Research judgment

**Reading Priority:** High. Suitable for Coding Agent, Terminal Agent, RL training and reliability engineering personnel.

**Confidence:** Medium to high. Key ablations, cross-harness results, and open artifacts support each other; high training costs, single-seed evaluation, and synthetic teacher dependencies still limit extrapolation.

**Follow-up observations:** Supplementing multi-seed confidence intervals; comparing the distribution of real failures to artificially injected failures; splitting the contributions of teacher quality, recovery tips, and queue freshness; checking in production-level long-running tasks that state snapshots are intact to save process, network, and external service side effects.
