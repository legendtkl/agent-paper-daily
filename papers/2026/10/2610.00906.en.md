## Conclusion summary

- ActiveSaddler keeps the underlying harness optimizer fixed and models the choice of the next failure-driven training batch as a non-stationary bandit with dynamically created failure-pattern arms.
- Under the same models, optimizer, and rollout budget, test Pass@1 reaches 59.8±1.0 on GAIA2 and 80.0±2.5 on Terminal-Bench 2.0, 4.4 and 7.5 points above a randomly fixed AutoSaddler order.
- Ablations support failure-pattern granularity, adaptive arm priority, and adaptive exploration. Applying the same curriculum to GEPA raises GAIA2 by 3.0 points.
- GAIA2 has 300 test tasks, three test executions, and a second independent optimization run. Terminal-Bench 2.0 has only 40 test tasks; the headline gap is three tasks and lacks a second full optimization run.
- The curriculum improves end-to-end cost–accuracy efficiency, but optimizer-side cost per generated patch is higher than AutoSaddler.

## Research question and background

Automatic harness optimization patches prompts, tools, middleware, and control logic from execution failures. Existing methods usually fix the training-scenario order in advance, even though scenario value changes as the harness repairs old weaknesses. Repeating solved failures wastes budget; moving on too early leaves repairable weaknesses unresolved.

ActiveSaddler isolates scenario selection as a separate optimization dimension. It asks whether an adaptive curriculum improves final harness quality without changing the patch generator.

## Method

The Failure-Pattern Extractor abstracts individual failures into symptoms and normalizes them into reusable arms. Category arms can merge unrelated weaknesses; per-scenario arms fragment a shared weakness. The Arm Prioritizer has an LLM score severity, fixability, breadth, and regression risk before softmax sampling. The Exploration Controller chooses DRAW for unseen scenarios or PULL for a known arm. Previously successful cases can re-enter exploration after all scenarios and arms have been covered, allowing regression rediscovery.

Train, development, and test splits are disjoint. Development performance selects the final harness; test data is reserved for reporting. The primary instantiation uses AutoSaddler, with an additional GEPA experiment.

## Experiments and evidence

GAIA2 uses 75 training, 65 development, and 300 test tasks; Terminal-Bench 2.0 uses 31/18/40. Shared rollout budgets are 1,400 and 490. Three test-time executions produce the reported means and standard deviations.

Default, fixed-order AutoSaddler, and ActiveSaddler score 53.6±1.1, 55.4±1.2, and 59.8±1.0 on GAIA2; the corresponding Terminal-Bench values are 64.2±2.9, 72.5±0.0, and 80.0±2.5.

Replacing failure-pattern arms with category or scenario arms costs 3.0/3.6 points on GAIA2 and 10.8/6.7 on Terminal-Bench. Removing the Arm Prioritizer costs 4.5/7.5 points; fixed exploration every five iterations costs 4.5/8.3. A second GAIA2 optimization run scores 61.6 and remains 5.0–5.7 points above fixed orders. GEPA rises from 54.2±2.2 to 57.2±0.4.

Optimizer-side cost per generated patch is USD 11.44 on GAIA2 and USD 11.37 on Terminal-Bench, versus USD 7.87 and USD 6.77 for AutoSaddler. Counting the five full-training-set evaluations required to build fixed difficulty orders, ActiveSaddler has a better end-to-end development-accuracy curve, but its curriculum calls are not individually cheaper.

## Limitations and risks

The authors state that two public/synthetic benchmarks do not establish production safety, privacy, governance, or compliance. The method has no independent mechanism for reward hacking, specification gaming, privilege misuse, unsafe commands, or sensitive-resource access. Production traces may contain PII, credentials, or proprietary content.

Our assessment is that the Terminal-Bench difference is only three of 40 tasks. Three test executions measure inference variability of one optimized harness, not optimization-process variance. Failure extraction, arm scores, and exploration all depend on LLM judgment and may create self-reinforcing calibration errors. Development-set selection also assumes that the development distribution represents deployment.

## Reproduction and application

The public implementation is on `feat/activesaddler`, pinned here at `93f3939001fc8d908ceb08e140c0e0bd430e81e0`, under MIT. It includes GAIA2 manifests, smoke/full profiles, curriculum state, and tests; Python 3.12–3.14 and `uv` are required.

The smoke profile uses six training tasks, one development task, two iterations, and paid OpenAI/Anthropic APIs. The full GAIA2 profile uses 75/65 train/dev tasks and GPT-5.5. Data and demo-filesystem revisions are pinned. The branch explicitly documents GAIA2 integration, while the Terminal-Bench paper result lacks an equally direct public reproduction path.

No paid optimization or full benchmark was rerun here. Repository structure and pinned artifacts establish availability, not independent reproduction.

## Related work

- [AutoSaddler](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23041) diagnoses traces and patches the harness; ActiveSaddler keeps that optimizer fixed and changes scenario selection.
- [GEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457) evolves prompts with reflection and Pareto search; the curriculum still adds 3.0 points when attached to GEPA.
- [Teacher-Student Curriculum Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.00183) schedules tasks by learning progress; ActiveSaddler dynamically creates targets from harness failures.
- [Curriculum Learning](https://doi.org/10.1145/1553374.1553380) established ordering as a learning variable; this paper applies the idea to non-parametric harness updates.
