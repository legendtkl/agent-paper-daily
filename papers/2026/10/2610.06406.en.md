## Conclusion summary

1. The paper breaks down the supervision problem of long-range agents into two things: identifying key decisions that require user confirmation, and connecting the judgments to traceable code or trace evidence, rather than read-only final responses.
2. AgentMonBench contains three sets of conditions: SpecGAP, SilentSwap and FeedbackTrace, which respectively check for missing requirements, implementations where the test still passes but the semantics have changed, and key decisions exposed by subsequent user feedback.
3. Training the irrelevant Evidence-Grounded Behavior Graph (EBG) first uses deterministic rules to organize evidence into behaviors, scopes and relationships, and then lets the monitoring model judge. Under most settings of the 8 models, EBG is better than directly reading the original context; but decision explanation is still significantly weaker than evidence location.
4. The evidence chain is complete: the construction process, Gold annotation, prompt words, results table, regression testing and reproduction instructions have all been made public. ## Research question and background

Long-range agents read files, invoke tools, modify repositories, and adjust experiments across multiple rounds. Users often only see plans and final conclusions, with the decisions that really affect the outcome scattered among intermediate trajectories and artifacts. Traditional task success rates cannot answer "whether the agent complies with incompletely stated requirements", "whether the implementation that seems to pass the test changes the semantics", "which independent choices should be suspended and the user confirmed".

The paper therefore defines the monitoring goal as: the monitor should both identify decisions that have a substantial impact on the interpretation of goals, constraints, or conclusions, and also provide evidence that can lead back to the original line of code, document paragraph, or tool event. This definition is stricter than general trajectory summarization, since correct summarization does not guarantee that key decisions are exposed, nor does retrieving relevant fragments guarantee that their consequences are understood.

## Method

**AgentMonBench.** SpecGAP controls the removal of key conditions from the DeNovoSWE task document and checks whether the monitor can retrieve unspecified constraints from the warehouse implementation that affect the results. SilentSwap replaces 5 implementation semantics while the existing tests still pass, and then confirms the changes with specialized semantic tests. FeedbackTrace selects the problems clearly pointed out by subsequent user feedback from the real interaction of SWE-chat, and only gives the track before feedback to the monitor.

**EBG.** Method does not train new models. For warehouse input, the deterministic builder combines conditions, operations, and results into Behaviors; for trajectory input, it combines requirements, actions, and responses into Behaviors. Related Behaviors are aggregated into Scopes and connected in explicit relationships with sources. The renderer then selects files according to task requirements, expands the one-hop neighborhood, or retains the trajectory order according to the task range, and finally allows the monitoring model to generate verification points from the structured evidence view.

**Differences from similar jobs.** RepoGraph mainly organizes the warehouse structure. Work such as Ask or Assume focuses on discovering information gaps before execution; EBG focuses on key decisions that have occurred during execution but have not been explicitly explained to the user, and requires that the output and original evidence can be traced in both directions.

## Experiments and evidence

**Data quality.** SpecGAP conducted a double mapping review on 484 conditions, and finally reached agreement on 447, with a rate of 92.4%; SilentSwap conducted a manual review of 500 replacements, and reached agreement on 468, with a rate of 93.6%; 93 out of 100 sessions of FeedbackTrace passed double review. Each of the three main evaluation groups retains 100 samples.

**Main results.** The paper compares Base, RepoGraph and EBG on 8 commercial and open weight models. Taking GPT-5.6 Sol as an example, SpecGAP's SF1 increased from Base 0.456 to 0.505, and LF1 increased from 0.501 to 0.569; SilentSwap's positioning score increased from 0.464 to 0.671; FeedbackTrace's evidence hit rate increased from 0.550 to 0.610. The gains of different models and indicators are not consistent, and individual judgment indicators have not improved.

**Failure analysis.** The paper breaks down failure into three levels: file selection, evidence presentation, and model judgment. After checking SpecGAP's 484 missing conditions, SilentSwap's 500 semantic substitutions, and FeedbackTrace's 100 verification points step by step, the author pointed out that many failures occurred when relevant evidence has appeared, indicating that the main bottleneck has shifted from "can you see it" to "can you understand its consequences." This is why higher retrieval scores cannot be equated with reliable supervision.

**Local artifact inspection.** Fixed repository commit `9f9d0dc25dfaa3ec3532ee1645f53db64b5ac53a` contains construction scripts, results, schema definitions, Codex Harness and reproduction instructions; `python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -t . -q` ran 335 tests that passed and 2 were skipped. The paid model is not called to regenerate the main table of the paper.

## Limitations and risks

**Explicit restrictions by the author.** Even if the current model uses EBG, there is still a lot of room for key decision identification and evidence interpretation; improvement in evidence location does not guarantee simultaneous improvement in decision-making understanding. The application part only has 5 real problem cases that have been controlled and rewritten, not an online randomized controlled experiment.

**Research inference.** Semantic score relies on GLM-5.2 and Qwen3.7-Max judge. The Pith machine review pointed out that the paper verified the Gold annotation, but did not fully prove that the judge's preferences for "decision importance" and "evidence sufficiency" are consistent with humans; structured output may also be more consistent with the judge's preferences. Feedback Gold comes from subsequent user corrections and naturally only covers problems that have been discovered and expressed by users.

## Reproduction and application

No credentials are required for offline graph building and regression testing; full prediction and judging require configuring model endpoints. The SilentSwap construct also requires Docker, the FeedbackTrace construct requires Python 3.12, and normal EBG usage requires Python 3.11 or above. The public data contains three sets of 100 samples each, Gold annotations and sample lists, and the large-scale original runs were not put directly into Git.

The most direct application in engineering is not to automatically block, but to generate evidence-based checks before plan execution and results reporting: listing unspecified constraints, changes that may be inconsistent with the original requirements, and choices that will change the interpretation of the conclusion, with code or trajectory locations. Treating EBG output as the final word still translates monitoring model understanding errors into new automation risks.

## Related work- **DeNovoSWE:** Provides detailed task documents, warehouses and runnable tests, which is the data basis of SpecGAP and SilentSwap; this article constructs supervision problems on it instead of continuing to measure the task completion rate.
- **SWE-chat:** Provides real coding interaction and subsequent user feedback; FeedbackTrace turns subsequent feedback into verification points in the previous trace.
- **RepoGraph:** Helps retrieval of code with a warehouse graph, which is the direct structured context baseline of the paper; EBG further expresses conditions, operations, results and task consequences.
- **Ask or Assume:** Check whether the Coding Agent first clarifies the specification when it is incomplete; EBG focuses on which independent decisions should still be exposed to the user for review after the Agent has acted.
