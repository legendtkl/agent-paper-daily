## Research question and background

Tool Agents can issue refunds, modify configurations, send files, or control devices. It is impossible to judge whether the Agent has checked the correct object, current status, authorization conditions, and dependencies before taking action by only looking at the interface's successful return or correct final status. An Agent may check Order A, but perform a refund on Order B; the final state is correct, but the observable trajectory does not support this action.

The paper defines this gap as the "evidence-action chain": each consequential action must be supported by evidence that has been established before the action and is bound to the correct entity and state; the multi-action workflow must also wait for the previous action to actually produce the required results. The research question therefore changes from "whether the action was successful" to "whether the Agent first formed verifiable evidence that is sufficient to support the action."

## Method

**Level 5 Agreement.** Legacy only requires that ALLOW/BLOCK/DEFER be given to fixed candidate actions; V0 requires that no external actions are generated after completing necessary investigations; V1 requires that evidence precedes a single correct action; V2 requires that subsequent actions in a linear workflow use real preorder results; V3 allows any legal topological order, but must satisfy the dependency graph.

**Evidence Ledger.** Evaluators bind observations to source interactions, entities and states. Only the facts corresponding to the target action are established; the same value appearing on other entities cannot replace the target evidence. In a multi-step task, the receipt or confirmation status of the previous action enters the evidence collection of the subsequent action.

**Certainty Rating.** Each case contains hidden evidence requirements, action specifications and dependencies. The evaluator only reads task context, actual tool trajectories and result status, checks survey completion, tools, goals, parameters, results and dependencies, does not read hidden reasoning, and does not call LLM judge. Exact Case Success requires that all conditions in the agreement are met simultaneously.

## Experiments and evidence

**Coverage.** 656 cases are distributed among Customer & Policy Operations 112, Engineering & Infrastructure 109, Legal & Finance 144, Research Assistance 97, Smart Home 96, Medical Operations 98; protocol distribution is Legacy 86, V0 175, V1 131, V2 132, V3 132. 5 model families use their native Harness and unified Inspect ReAct Harness respectively, totaling 10 combination; each case-combination was run 3 times, and the main evaluation coverage was 100%.

**Main results.** Claude-5/Claude Code has the highest overall ECS at 67.2%; GLM-5.2/ZCode has 97.7% on Legacy, but V1, V2, and V3 are only 32.1%, 12.1%, and 34.1%. The corresponding three items of DeepSeek-V4/DSH are about 59% to 65%, indicating that static permission judgment cannot replace the investigation, timing and dependency execution in real interactions.

**Failed location.** In V0, "stop before necessary investigation is completed" is 21.7% ~ 62.9%; in V1 episodes where action has been attempted, "act before evidence is complete" is 37.0% ~ 66.9%. The single-action success rate after complete evidence reaches 93.2% to 100% in 9/10 combinations, indicating that the main bottleneck is more often located in the timing of investigation and action, rather than the tool invocation itself.

**Controlled intervention.** After removing one decisive record from 43 V1 cases, the action probabilities of the three combinations dropped by 37.2 to 45.2 percentage points, but there were still 46.5% to 53.5% of completed episode actions. Providing the agent with a user-side statement that "the record has been checked and meets the conditions" inhibits action more strongly than simply presenting missing evidence, revealing an imbalance in the model's weighting of requester wording versus verifiable evidence.

**Evaluator validation with local checks.** 656/656 author reference solutions passed the evaluator; the blind review of 120 cases reached κ = 0.87 for action support judgment; in the audit of 300 model trajectories, the false acceptance rate was 2.0% and the false rejection rate was 1.3%. ## Limitations and risks

**Explicit restrictions by the author.** Benchmarks use synthetic tasks, fictitious entities, and controlled policies and are not to be interpreted as security certification of real financial, medical, smart home, or infrastructure systems. The evidence ledger describes observable support, rather than which piece of evidence is actually relied upon within the model; evidence intervention on multi-action protocols remains for follow-up research.

**Research inference.** Hidden specifications solidify "what you must know before taking action" as the author's definition. Policy conflicts, authority delegation, and information timeliness in real business are more complex. The comparison of native Harness and Inspect fixed the case, but still mixed the prompting, tool rendering, runtime recovery and model adaptation differences, and the entire difference cannot be attributed to a single Harness mechanism.

**Intervention boundaries.** The requester's statement of reducing the probability of action does not mean that the model has formed a correct view of evidence; failure to act may also be overly conservative or operation failure. In the SCGR analysis, structured evidence selection significantly improves some combinations, but decreases it for other combinations, indicating that formatted evidence itself is not a universal fix.

## Reproduction and application

The public repository provides 656 cases, 6 environments, tool schemas, deterministic evaluators, simulation portals and OpenRouter running portals. The core runner uses Python 3.11 or above and the standard library; the code is MIT and the data is CC BY 4.0. The complete master table still needs to correspond to the business model and Harness, and cost, version and availability will affect re-runs.

Engineering applications can put the Evidence Ledger mechanism before external writing, payment, permission modification and cross-system operations: recording target entities, necessary conditions, evidence sources, evidence time and previous results; missing access control checks, wrong entities and unfinished dependencies. It is suitable as an audit and blocking signal and should not solely replace domain policy or manual approval.

## Related work- **τ²-Bench (arXiv:2506.07982):** Evaluate the Agent and the user to jointly advance the task in a dual-control dialogue environment; SafeActBench puts more emphasis on the source of evidence and dependencies before each external action.
- **ToolSandbox:** Provides stateful tool calling environment and executable evaluation; SafeActBench adds evidence timing and entity binding to such final state inspection.
- **AppWorld:** Evaluate cross-application interactive task completion; SafeActBench does not use end-state completion as the only criterion, but audits whether actions are supported by observed evidence.
- **Coding Agents Don’t Know When to Act (arXiv:2605.07769):** Study the timing of action of Coding Agent; this article is extended to 6 business domains, single action and multi-action dependencies.
- **Agent-SafetyBench (arXiv:2412.14470):** Covers Agent security risks; SafeActBench focuses on whether the evidence is sufficient to support specific consequential actions.
