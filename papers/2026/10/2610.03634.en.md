## Conclusion summary

- DepGPO builds a command-dependency graph from terminal read/write traces, traces backward from verifier-read resources, and reallocates trajectory advantage to relevant writes and their supporting reads.
- It ranks first in all eight Qwen3.5-9B/Qwen3.6-27B, SETA/TMAX, and Terminal-Bench 2.0/2.1 combinations: 4.34–14.53 points above GRPO and 3.22–10.03 above the strongest other RL baseline.
- Dependency analysis adds about 3.8 seconds per trajectory, 7% wall-clock time per update, and 1% peak GPU memory, without extra model forward passes.
- The proxy depends on verifier-visible reads and stdout value matching; 17.6% of trajectories fall back, while code, data, weights, and a pinned environment are not public.

## Research question and background

Terminal-agent rewards usually state whether an entire trajectory succeeded, not which commands influenced verification. Existing step-level schemes use position or model judgment but do not reconstruct file, environment-variable, and stdout dependencies. The paper asks whether propagating credit only along observable dependency paths reduces irrelevant training signal.

## Method

Execution tracing extracts resource reads and writes into a directed graph. Backward traversal from the verifier read set identifies relevant writes and supporting reads. DepGPO redistributes group-relative trajectory advantage over those steps while keeping the GRPO objective. If no usable graph or path exists, it falls back to trajectory-level credit.

Unlike positional decay, LLM step scoring, or final-step reward, the method uses an execution-time causal proxy and adds no model calls. It remains observational rather than an interventional causal test.

## Experiments and evidence

The authors train Qwen3.5-9B and Qwen3.6-27B on SETA and TMAX and evaluate on Terminal-Bench 2.0 and 2.1. Each task gets five independent attempts for Pass@1; the full evaluation is repeated three times for mean±SD.

Ablations removing relevant reads, relevant writes, or command dependencies reduce performance. Usable graphs appear for 82.4% of training trajectories; 17.6% fall back. The three repetitions are final evaluations and do not establish three independent training seeds for every setting.

## Limitations and risks

- The implementation depends on verifier-visible resources; hidden state, network side effects, and implicit cross-process dependencies can be missed.
- Typed-value matching for stdout can introduce equal-value false matches or miss semantic dependencies.
- The study covers terminal tasks and two related Terminal-Bench versions, not browser, GUI, or production environments.
- Without code, splits, weights, and a pinned environment, the paper can be checked internally but not independently rerun.

## Reproduction and application

Reproduction requires command-level read/write tracing, verifier-read records, SETA/TMAX training sets, both Qwen bases, and Terminal-Bench 2.0/2.1. The method best fits file-state terminal agents with explicit verifiers. Network services, database transactions, and hidden state require richer resource graphs and intervention checks.

## Related work

- [TMAX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23321) supplies terminal RL tasks and baselines; DepGPO changes credit rather than task generation.
- [GraphGPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26684) adds graph structure to policy optimization; DepGPO derives its graph from execution dependencies.
- [Granularity-Adaptive Credit Assignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12424) adapts credit granularity; DepGPO weights steps through verifier-relevant paths.
- [PHGPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13691) is a hierarchical policy baseline; this paper emphasizes concrete execution chains.
