# OSReward

English | [中文](2607.28609.md)

> Primary category: [`EV`](../../../docs/categories.en.md#ev); secondary categories: [`CU`](../../../docs/categories.en.md#cu), [`SS`](../../../docs/categories.en.md#ss)

## Summary

- The paper evaluates 27 VLM judges on 1,019 human gold-standard trajectories across platforms, directly testing the reliability of reward signals for computer-use agents.
- A hard set reveals a widespread leniency bias: judges often mistake an agent's claim of success for actual success in the environment state.
- The open OS-Shepherd model improves failure recognition, while the paper reports an API-equivalent cost roughly 30–60 times lower than closed alternatives.
- Data construction required about 800 person-hours; cross-benchmark conclusions still depend partly on existing verifiers.

## Research Question and Background

Computer-use-agent training and evaluation often rely on vision-language models as reward models. If a reward model cannot reliably distinguish actual environmental success from a verbal claim of success, both training signals and final evaluation will be systematically inflated.

## Core Method

The authors build human gold-standard trajectories across platforms, create a hard subset specifically exposing leniency bias, and compare multiple classes of VLM judges under a shared setup. They then train the open OS-Shepherd reward model and examine cost and transfer performance.

## Experiments and Evidence

Key scale figures include 1,019 human trajectories, 284 hard examples, and 27 judges. The primary evidence concerns differences in failure recognition on the hard set and the open model's performance at lower cost. Screenshots were not migrated with the historical record; exact figures should be checked against the paper tables.

## Limitations and Risks

Human data is expensive, and platform coverage and task distribution may limit generalization. Cross-benchmark comparisons depend on existing verifiers, whose own biases may propagate.

## Reproducibility and Application

The work can serve as a reward-model regression set, a failure-recognition test set, and a reference for training-data quality audits in computer-use agents.

## Community Assessment

The historical collection verified only the official project page and activity on the Hugging Face dataset. It did not preserve verifiable third-party interaction metrics, so community consensus cannot be inferred.

## Research Assessment

The work directly quantifies reward-model misclassification risk and has greater engineering value than work that only raises task success rates. Follow-up should examine data and model availability, cross-platform transfer, and independent reproduction.
