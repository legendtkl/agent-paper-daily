## Conclusion summary

- The paper audits four agent-safety benchmarks as measurement instruments and finds that they cannot be substituted for one general safety score.
- R-Judge's F1 metric rewards an always-unsafe classifier with 0.690, above five of the 21 models that produce discriminating outputs.
- A -0.64 relationship on a seven-model panel becomes +0.02 on the common 18-model panel; small panels can manufacture apparent trade-offs.
- AgentHarm correlates +0.72 with jailbreak safety after capability control, but both measure harmful compliance. This supports convergent validity, not general safety.

## Research question and background

Agent benchmarks separately target prompt-injection resistance, harmful-task refusal, risk awareness, and safe behavior in tool environments, yet their scores are often collapsed into general safety rankings. The paper separates construct validity, metric validity, and criterion validity: whether benchmarks target one property, whether official metrics reward degenerate behavior, and whether scores predict held-out outcomes beyond capability.

## Method

The authors rerun R-Judge, InjecAgent, AgentHarm, and AgentDojo with official implementations and scorers. They use 571, 300, 44, and 100 items, covering 21, 22, 19, and 5 valid models respectively; the common broad-coverage panel has 18 models.

Capability anchors are a fixed 500-item MMLU subset and 198 GPQA-Diamond items measured under one harness. Held-out criteria include 40 τ²-bench retail tasks, fictional agent-misalignment probes, and three jailbreak outcomes. Analyses use Spearman and partial correlations, bootstrap, organization-clustered resampling, leave-one-organization-out checks, and internal consistency. The plan is described as prespecified, not preregistered, because it lacks an independent timestamp.

## Experiments and evidence

**Metric validity.** R-Judge labels 52.7% of items unsafe. Under F1, an all-positive classifier scores `2π/(1+π)=0.690`, beating five genuinely discriminating models. The paper recommends full confusion structure, specificity, and balanced accuracy.

**Panel stability.** R-Judge specificity versus AgentHarm safety correlates -0.64 on seven models but +0.02 on the common 18-model panel. Roughly one quarter of random seven-model subsets reach `|ρ| ≥ 0.5` around that near-zero full-panel value. Disagreeing rankings do not establish a stable safety-capability trade-off.

**Criterion validity.** Capability correlates +0.60 with task success and -0.44 with misalignment safety. On the paired 20-model panel the contrast is `Δ=-1.00`, 95% CI `[-1.48,-0.49]`, `p < 0.001`, surviving leave-one-organization-out and clustered bootstrap checks. On an expanded roughly 41-model panel, capability-misalignment weakens to -0.16 and jailbreak becomes +0.34; neither is significant.

AgentHarm's capability-controlled correlation with three-template jailbreak safety is +0.72 and remains +0.66 with another judge. Both measure harmful compliance, while the organization-resampled selectivity test is `p=0.051`; the warranted claim is convergent validity for a related construct.

## Limitations and risks

**Author-stated limitations.** The common panel has only 18 models; MMLU and GPQA are static anchors while a BFCL pilot was confounded by format compliance; some multiple comparisons lack family-wise correction; AgentDojo covers only five models; held-out criteria are not deployment-harm probabilities.

**Research inference.** The paper rejects interchangeable safety scores but does not provide one replacement. Commercial APIs, historical models, and LLM judges complicate reproduction. The inaccessible artifact package makes public verifiability weaker than the manuscript suggests.

## Reproduction and application

Full reproduction requires four official benchmarks, APIs for up to 22 models from nine organizations, one MMLU/GPQA harness, three external criteria, and organization-level resampling. The paper gives panel coverage, sample sizes, score orientation, and robustness checks, but not an artifact URL, API snapshots, or costs.

A useful reporting unit is “benchmark + metric + target behavior + model panel.” Binary safety claims should include the full confusion matrix and balanced accuracy; cross-version rankings need broad capability coverage and organization/judge sensitivity checks.

## Related work

- [AgentHarm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09024): harmful agentic task compliance; the audit supports association with a related jailbreak construct only.
- [AgentDojo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13352): prompt-injection security and utility in tool environments; this audit has only five models.
- [InjecAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02691): direct and indirect prompt-injection evaluation; treated here as a distinct construct.
- [τ²-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07982): dual-control conversational-agent success; its retail subset is a held-out task criterion.
- [Measuring What Matters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04703): construct-validity methods for LLM benchmarks, adapted here to agent safety.
