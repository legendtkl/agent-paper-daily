## Conclusion summary

- PAST-Bench asks not only whether cross-session scores improve, but whether gains follow the intended save, retrieve, and update path. It contains 26 task families and 204 fresh-session episodes across memory, procedural reuse, information gathering, and update.
- With Hermes fixed, all seven base models gain from persistence but on different capabilities. With MiniMax-M2.7 fixed, four agent frameworks range from -0.08 to +0.13 overall, so persistence does not imply stable improvement.
- Hermes+ adds Plan, Render, Route, Gate, and Close mechanisms. Its persistence-on score remains 0.66, while the gap rises from +0.13 to +0.15 and mechanism evidence from 0.64 to 0.73. Update rises from +0.12 to +0.24, but Procedural falls from +0.05 to -0.02.
- The change is costly: Hermes+ averages 31,859 tokens per MiniMax episode, about 2.5 times Hermes's 12,615, although wall time rises only about 1.10 times.
- Score: 91/100. The benchmark improves the evidence standard for persistent agents, but tasks are synthetic and isolated, and mechanism evidence is consistency evidence rather than causal proof.

## Research question and background

Personal agents retain preferences, histories, tool routines, and skills across sessions. Typical evaluations compare final scores with and without memory but cannot establish whether retained state caused the gain or distinguish model capability, framework design, and shortcuts. PAST-Bench defines improvement as better later fresh-session behavior under matched persistence controls with artifacts and telemetry consistent with the expected mechanism.

## Method

Each family has paired persistence-on and persistence-off runs. Model, agent, prompt, tools, context window, output limit, and compaction policy remain fixed; only access to prior state changes. The 26 families comprise five Memory, eight Procedural Reuse, six Information Gathering, and seven Update families, totaling 204 episodes. Results report both task-score gaps and mechanism evidence.

Hermes+ maps five trace failures to runtime interventions: Plan consults state before planning; Render presents typed, scoped, versioned bindings; Route stores workflows as ranked, patchable skills; Gate forces retrieval before history-dependent actions; Close synchronously replaces stale state at episode end.

## Experiments and evidence

With Hermes fixed, GLM-5.1 reaches an overall persistence gap of +0.20 and Kimi K2.6 +0.17; all seven models improve overall, but their strongest capabilities differ. With MiniMax-M2.7 fixed, nanobot, ZeroClaw, Agent-Zero, and Hermes score +0.13, +0.12, -0.08, and +0.13, while mechanism evidence is 0.57, 0.55, 0.39, and 0.64.

Single-mechanism ablations align with target diagnoses: Render gives the best Memory score (0.80), Route the largest Procedural gap (+0.10), Gate the largest Information gap (+0.17), and Close the largest Update gap (+0.16). Full Hermes+ raises the overall gap from +0.13 to +0.15 and mechanism evidence from 0.64 to 0.73, but Procedural regresses to -0.02.

Hermes+ improves or preserves three of five model baselines; DeepSeek-V4-Pro and Claude Opus 4.6 regress slightly. Three-run MiniMax results are 0.13±0.04 for Hermes and 0.15±0.06 for Hermes+, making the 0.02 difference smaller than run-to-run variation. Hermes+ is better read as a diagnostic scaffold than a proven universal improvement.

## Limitations and risks

The authors state that task families are synthetic and isolated, without long sequences, cross-family interference, real-user interaction, or stronger recursive improvement. The mechanism score tests consistency with an expected path rather than causal necessity. Hermes+ effects are model- and capability-dependent.

The paper's “recursive self-improvement” means later behavioral gains from retained external state, not weight updates or self-redesign. Open-ended grading uses MiniMax-M2.7; human validation and sensitivity checks help but cannot remove all judge bias. Three-run variance covers only two MiniMax configurations.

## Reproduction and application

The Apache-2.0 repository includes Python 3.11+ code, Docker sandboxes, all 26 families, five agent adapters, configs, mock services, and tests. Full runs require Docker, provider APIs, and a MiniMax judge. GitHub issue #1 tracks a Hugging Face dataset release, so distribution is still being completed.

The most valuable practice is to require later improvement in a fresh session, verify that state was written and read at the correct time, and test whether corrections replace stale state. Retrieval gates and synchronous closeout can transfer to assistants, coding agents, and research agents, but token cost and over-retrieval need explicit budgets.

## Related work

- [MemGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560) frames memory as an operating system; PAST-Bench measures its longitudinal utility and traces.
- [SkillsBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12670) evaluates static skills; this benchmark focuses on sequential save, retrieval, and update.
- [Recursive Harness Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15524) evolves harnesses; PAST-Bench adds matched controls and mechanism evidence.
- [PATH-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01149) studies experience paths, transfer, and forgetting.
- [ContinualSkillBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03874) compares independent, pure-ICL, and explicit skill maintenance and similarly questions skill-count growth as a proxy for capability.
