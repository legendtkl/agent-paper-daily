## Conclusion summary

- A successful but unsafe trajectory can be distilled into a persistent skill and cause harm after the triggering input disappears.
- SkillMisevo-Gym separates authoring, storage, retrieval, and execution; SkillMisevo-Bench uses nine lifecycle metrics across four agent frameworks and multiple evolution methods.
- Each of 25 agent-method configurations runs 25 episodes and 525 tasks. All 21 evolving settings author unsafe artifacts, 19 retrieve and propagate them, and 15 cause fresh-session harm.
- Three malicious tasks raise carryover attack success from 16.0% to 35.3%, while useful reuse also rises.
- SafeEvolve cuts unsafe retrieval by 26.7 points and fresh-session harm by 17.3 points with a 0.4-point mean utility change, but evidence is limited to English coding and computer-use sandboxes.

## Research question and background

Existing safety benchmarks usually test current behavior or static artifacts. Self-improving agents convert successful traces into executable procedures, workflows, or memory. This paper asks whether unsafe experience is authored into a skill, retrieved later, and then causes harm in a clean session.

## Method

SkillMisevo-Gym isolates dialogue state from an episode-scoped persistent skill store. Frozen streams contain malicious learning tasks, concept-aligned benign tasks, and persistence probes. SkillMisevo-Bench fixes task IDs, schedules, seeds, judges, and denominators and measures nine lifecycle outcomes. SafeEvolve audits candidate skills and lineage, minimally deletes or narrows dangerous fragments, and governs ranking, retirement, and capacity eviction at reuse time.

## Experiments and evidence

RQ1 covers Claude Code, Codex, Hermes, and OpenClaw with EvoSkill, AutoSkill, SkillClaw, SkillsVote, and SkillOpt. Each condition contains 225 malicious learning tasks, 225 benign evaluations, and 75 persistence tasks. All 21 evolving conditions author unsafe artifacts, 19 show unsafe retrieval and contamination, and 15 retain fresh-session harm. After three malicious tasks, carryover ASR rises from 16.0% to 35.3% and reaches 41.3% at full budget; carryover utility rises from 30.0% to 55.3%. SafeEvolve reduces unsafe retrieval and carryover harm by 26.7 and 17.3 points, respectively, with only a 0.4-point mean utility change.

## Limitations and risks

The authors limit evidence to English coding and computer-use tasks, file-based skill state, short episode horizons, and hosted models. Parameter updates, multimodal memory, multilingual use, and natural long-term deployments remain untested. Research inference: synthetic identities and inert endpoints support causal measurement but may not represent organic contamination; shared judge biases remain possible; SafeEvolve is a control layer, not a proof of safety.

## Reproduction and application

The paper fixes episode schedules, task counts, denominators, Docker isolation, and upstream licenses. Each condition runs 525 tasks without model training; only the skill store persists. Code is public, but the repository had no stars, forks, or detected license at collection time. Production systems should treat skill updates like code releases: retain provenance, scan writes, constrain tools and targets, version state, and run benign plus persistence regressions.
