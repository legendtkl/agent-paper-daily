## Conclusion summary

- AutoDesign makes the harness an outer-loop optimization target: a meta-optimizer aggregates rollout failures and asks a coding agent to make bounded updates.
- The 100-paper, five-discipline PosterBench Main Track gives AutoDesign 78.32, 7.45 points above Claude Design.
- Attaching the same DesignHarness across seven model/coding-agent configurations raises the mean score from 54.99 to 67.39, with gains of 5.01–19.56 points.
- A full run uses 253 tool calls and 11 editing turns in under 40 minutes for less than $3, but evidence is limited to paper-to-poster generation.
- Autonomous optimization plateaus at 80.88 and reaches 88.39 only after human redirection, exposing a local-optimum problem.

## Research question and background

Most design agents keep prompts, tools, and editing workflows fixed and optimize only one artifact. AutoDesign asks whether a meta-optimizer can convert repeated rollout evidence into persistent harness changes that transfer across papers, models, and coding-agent configurations.

## Method

The inner loop ingests paper metadata, sections, passages, and visuals, creates an editable artifact, checks render integrity, applies visual critique, and makes localized code edits. The outer loop aggregates failures across papers, proposes a bounded update to one harness component, and evaluates the revised harness. Optimization-time feedback is separated from the frozen PosterBench evaluator.

## Experiments and evidence

PosterBench scores faithfulness, coverage, density, visual evidence, layout, readability, and aesthetics, with hard ceilings for severe failures. It includes a 100-paper Main Track and a fixed 10-paper mini split. AutoDesign scores 78.32 versus Claude Design's 70.87. Across seven controlled configurations, DesignHarness raises the mean from 54.99 to 67.39; the strongest Codex setting rises from 75.87 to 81.46. A system-blind human study supports the ranking signal, although it remains poster-specific.

## Limitations and risks

The authors limit the claim to one artifact type and one selected harness component, acknowledge VLM-based evaluation, and show that the autonomous search requires human redirection after plateauing. Research inference: the optimizer may learn PosterBench-specific preferences; closed-system comparisons cannot fully normalize tools and budgets; automated harness edits require versioning, regression tests, and rollback gates.

## Reproduction and application

The repository releases DesignHarness, PosterBench, and example workflows. At collection time it had 37 stars and 2 forks, with a non-standard license reported by GitHub. Reproduction requires a long-horizon coding agent, rendering/browser tools, a visual critic, and API budget. The safer deployment pattern limits updates to auditable components and requires reviewed, reversible changes.

## Related work

- [Evo-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09096) evaluates automatic harness improvement; AutoDesign is a domain-specific end-to-end instance.
- [Automated Design of Agentic Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08435) searches agent structures; AutoDesign emphasizes code harnesses and editable artifacts.
- [SWE-agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15793) shows that agent-computer interfaces affect performance; AutoDesign makes that layer learnable.
- [OpenHands](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16741) provides a general coding-agent execution layer that can sit beneath such a design harness.
