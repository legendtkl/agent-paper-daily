## Conclusion summary

- The paper frames unbounded coding-agent instruction growth as catastrophic remembering: adding a rule is cheap, but safely removing it requires recovering why it was added.
- The observational study covers 1,867 GitHub repositories, 299,440 file transitions, and 247,694 instruction lifetimes. Instruction files grow 226% over their lifetime and gain 4.9 net instructions per commit; older instructions are less likely to be deleted.
- An inverted IFEval experiment separates an instruction from its rationale. At 51 steps, excess size is +211.3% without comments and +1.4% with informative comments, while both arms reach 44.0% constraint satisfaction.
- In 64 WildIFEval worlds, 16 noisy rules reduce satisfaction of valid rules by 24.1 percentage points. Informative comments raise satisfaction from 50.4% to 62.0%, but this result depends on an LLM judge rather than executable ground truth.
- The evidence supports recording rationale and outcomes for agent instructions. It does not support automatic deletion: the protocol empties 12.1% of worlds, and the author explicitly recommends a human deletion gate for safety-sensitive rules.

## Research question and background

CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md, and copilot-instructions.md are repeatedly loaded by coding agents. Teams usually append a rule after a failure but do not preserve the triggering failure, hypothesis, or validation outcome. Later maintainers see what to do but not why, making deletion risky.

The paper contrasts staleness, content fragility, and imperfect recall through their opposing predictions for deletion hazard, then tests whether preserving rationale changes prompt growth in controlled environments.

## Method

The observational study reconstructs version histories of public agent instruction files, matches instructions across revisions, and estimates deletion hazard. Five pre-registered criteria test whether rewrites, migrations, content, and author composition explain the age effect.

The controlled study inverts IFEval: published instructions become a hidden minimum cover, their verifiers become latent constraints, and the maintainer sees only a short objective. The control passes instructions only; a placebo passes comment-shaped noise; treatment comments record failures, hypotheses, recurrence, and outcomes. The harness strips comments before execution, isolating their effect on future maintainers.

The WildIFEval study seeds four or five true rules plus 16 distractors and measures constraint satisfaction after three maintenance rounds. An arm-blind judge sees only one constraint and one response; a second judge checks directional robustness.

## Experiments and evidence

The corpus contains 1,801 multi-version files, 299,440 transitions, and 247,694 instruction spells; 28,426 end in deletion. Files grow 226% over their lifetime, add 4.9 net instructions per commit, and show a deletion log-hazard slope of -0.032 per commit.

Across 552 fifteen-step histories, excess sizes for no comments, noisy comments, and informative comments are +60.4%, +53.2%, and -5.8%; final satisfaction rates are 39.0%, 40.3%, and 38.2%. At 51 steps, each arm uses 184 worlds but one seed: excess sizes are +211.3%, +147.9%, and +1.4%, with no accuracy trade-off.

On WildIFEval, 16 noisy rules reduce satisfaction from 65.6% to 41.5%, a -24.1-point difference with 95% CI [-33.4, -14.9]. Informative comments raise three-round satisfaction from 50.4% to 62.0%, +11.6 points with 95% CI [5.1, 18.3]. A second judge estimates +7.8 points; the between-judge difference interval includes zero.

## Limitations and risks

**Author-stated boundaries.** Rewrite and migration rules censor 77.3% of deaths. The matcher is validated on only 50 hand-labeled transitions, and segmentation is not systematically varied. Controlled worlds have two or three minimum instructions, 15 or 51 steps, and the same model as maintainer and executor. WildIFEval uses LLM judging.

**Research inference.** One author performs the hand annotation, and the key 51-step run has one seed. The corpus lacks language, programming-language, and domain distributions, so it does not establish results for non-English instructions, system prompts, or full skills. Treating a comment as permission to delete is the largest deployment risk.

## Reproduction and application

The paper reports 49,680 model calls, 37.9M tokens, and 45 model-hours for the canonical controlled campaign, plus 8-vCPU, 16-GiB CPU containers. Model snapshots and varying decoding settings are pinned. However, no verifiable artifact URL was available at collection time.

A low-risk operational use is to attach the triggering failure, hypothesis, validation outcome, recurrence count, and scope to every new instruction. Execution can receive only the operational rule; maintenance can retrieve the rationale. Deletion should require review, regression tasks, and a reversible commit.
