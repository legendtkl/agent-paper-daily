# Adaptive Adversaries: A Multi-Turn, Multi-LLM Benchmark for LLM Agent Security

English | [中文](2607.18063.md)

> Primary category: [`SS`](../../../docs/categories.en.md#ss); secondary category: [`EV`](../../../docs/categories.en.md#ev)

## Summary

The benchmark lets attacking agents observe defense responses and adapt their strategies in subsequent turns, testing whether fixed attack pools overestimate agents' robustness to prompt injection.

## Experiments and Evidence

- 21 evaluation scenarios and 10 public development scenarios, together with tooling for multi-attacker orchestration.
- With the same attacker, defender, and scoring setup, first-turn attack success rates are 0%–1% and rise to 5.4%–14.0% after allowing 15 rounds of adaptation.
- Three frontier attack models find 1.4–2.2 times as many distinct successful attacks as the strongest individual attacker.

## Limitations and Risks

The experiments target defenders without cross-turn memory and do not directly generalize to systems with state, policy updates, or external permission isolation. Security rankings are sensitive to the scenarios, while attack-round and model budgets also change the conclusions.

## Community and Reproducibility

The paper states that the benchmark, harness, CLI, and evaluation transcripts are open. This retrospective did not preserve timestamped third-party community interactions, so it records only the materials released by the authors.

## Research Assessment

The work shows that security evaluations need attackers that adapt to defense behavior. In engineering practice, model-level defenses should still be evaluated separately from permission controls, effect checks, and runtime isolation.
