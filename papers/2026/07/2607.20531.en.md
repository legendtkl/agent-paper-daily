# DynamicMCPBench: A Trace-Grounded, Effect-Scored Benchmark for LLM Agents over Live MCP Servers

English | [中文](2607.20531.md)

> Primary category: [`EV`](../../../docs/categories.en.md#ev); secondary categories: [`TU`](../../../docs/categories.en.md#tu), [`SY`](../../../docs/categories.en.md#sy)

## Summary

DynamicMCPBench executes successful trajectories against live, stateful MCP services and compresses them into effect checkpoints independent of the specific call path. It scores whether the expected environmental change occurred rather than the final answer or a unique tool sequence.

## Experiments and Evidence

- 24 models, 121 MCP servers, and 750 tasks across 15 classes of tool-use challenges.
- Uses `pass^3`, requiring all three independent runs to succeed; no model solves 31% of the tasks.
- Accuracy falls from 39% for the shortest tool chains to 13% for the longest; chance-corrected agreement with human verification is 0.76.

## Limitations and Risks

Live services change over time, increasing realism while reducing exact reproducibility. `pass^3` measures stable success and should not be conflated with `pass@3`, which permits one successful retry.

## Community and Reproducibility

The paper presents the work as a framework that can be rerun on users' own servers and tasks. This retrospective did not preserve verifiable third-party discussion links or interaction snapshots and therefore does not infer popularity.

## Research Assessment

The work is useful for testing whether MCP agents actually produce external effects and for exposing stability problems in long tool chains. Adoption requires pinning service versions, initial state, retry policies, and verifier versions.
