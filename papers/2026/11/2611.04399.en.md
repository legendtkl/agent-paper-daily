## Conclusion summary

- An audit of HAL and tau2-bench finds four silent instrument errors: episodes that never reach grading, nested calls counted twice, grader actions stored as agent actions, and re-grades that rebuild the wrong environment.
- HAL Generalist trails Tool-Calling by 12.7 pooled points over nine shared models as published; applying the same grader to the ungraded episodes yields 0.414 versus 0.414.
- 1,596 of 2,334 airline records, or 68.4%, contain grader-injected gold actions. In 58 records the agent makes no tool call while the archive displays the complete gold sequence.
- HAL maintainers independently confirm all three HAL issues. Openness is necessary for finding these errors, but rerunning released code can still reproduce a stable, wrong number.

## Research question and background

Agent leaderboards usually attribute a score to a model, harness, task, and grader without separately auditing the instrument that terminates, logs, prices, replays, and versions the run. Components that are locally sensible can interact to fail silently.

This paper does not ask whether the reward rule is ideal. It asks whether the published pipeline applied its own rule, recorded the agent's behavior, priced calls correctly, and preserved comparability across versions.

## Method

The author re-derives results from public HAL and tau2-bench trajectories, leaderboards, pinned source versions, and changelogs without running agents or calling models. HAL provenance is reconstructed from the agent's own LLM outputs and user-simulator calls, rather than inferred from action position.

For tau2-bench, fixed trajectories are crossed with multiple evaluator and task-data versions. This isolates evaluator changes, task-data changes, and a solo-mode detection bug.

The paper proposes five cheap checks: report the graded fraction; aggregate cost from root spans and print the multiplier; tag each action's producer; pin evaluator and task-data versions with per-task re-grade diffs; and ship machine-readable errata inside frozen archives.

## Experiments and evidence

- Only 51.2% of 613 HAL Generalist records with non-terminal gold actions are graded. Completing the same grading raises pooled accuracy from 0.300 to 0.426. Across the nine shared models, Generalist versus Tool-Calling moves from 0.282 versus 0.409 to 0.414 versus 0.414.
- 1,596 of 2,334 airline records contain injected actions: 73 interior-only and 92 both interior and suffix. Suffix-only cleaning misses them.
- For 1,259 records with at least two non-terminal gold actions, verbatim replay matches the recorded reward 70.3% of the time; removing injected actions raises agreement to 91.7%, or 94.0% with embedded gold.
- The tau2-bench 1.0.1 banking re-grade reverses 4 of 136 pairwise orderings among 17 submissions. Airline changes flip 77 of 800 simulations. A solo-mode bug drops four telecom files by 59–82 points without raising an error.
- At commit `52fee2ff954849794527312a53f15ec85667877b`, rerunning `tables.py` on released `records_v2.csv` reproduces 2,334, 51.2%, 0.426, 68.4%, and 70.3%→91.7%. All reproduction scripts compile.

## Limitations and risks

The evidence covers two leaderboards. The expectation of similar failures elsewhere is a mechanism-based inference, not a field-wide survey. HAL re-grading stubs the user simulator, so it checks database and output criteria rather than full conversation dynamics.

Generalist and Tool-Calling still differ in budgets and policy access. Closing the pooled gap does not establish scaffold equivalence; model cells contain only about 40–82 records and cannot resolve small differences.

The initial disclosure overstated the Generalist graded fraction and corrected score as 39.3% and 0.468. After a HAL notice exposed interior gold injection, the author corrected them to 51.2% and 0.426; the paper uses the corrected values. The correction improves credibility and demonstrates that audits themselves need provenance and regression tests.

There is no root license. A full rerun requires 380 encrypted HAL archives, the public passphrase, pinned tau-bench code, several tau2-bench releases, and large trajectory files. This review recomputes released derived tables rather than regenerating the CSV from raw archives.

## Reproduction and application

The package documents exact inputs and commands. HAL reproduction needs the 380 archives and tau-bench commit `14bf0ef...`; tau2-bench needs releases 0.2.0, 1.0.0, pre-1.0.1, and 1.0.1 plus 30–95 MB trajectory files.

This review reruns the derived-table computation and static checks but does not redownload raw archives. The strongest independent evidence is HAL's maintainer confirmation and the resulting leaderboard notices. The tau-bench and tau2-bench issues had no maintainer response at submission time.

Operationally, every leaderboard cell should expose grading coverage, a cost-deduplication multiplier, evaluator and task-data versions, and producer-tagged actions. A re-grade should fail when replay divergence is excessive instead of returning a plausible score.

## Community response

HAL maintainers state in discussion #9 that they reproduced and confirmed all three HAL issues on their own archive, then added cost, lower-bound, and trajectory-cleaning notices. This is strong independent evidence, though it does not cover the two tau2-bench findings.

On The Wire distinguishes the paper's corrected results from the earlier disclosure: the early broad per-model reordering claim does not hold, while the pooled gap closure, one model-level order change, and other instrument failures remain. The article is AI-edited but links the primary discussion, issues, pull requests, and changelog.

PaperPeel argues that the paper's contribution is realized evidence for an already-known need to audit evaluation infrastructure. It does not execute the reproduction package.

The Hugging Face Papers API does not index the paper; Hacker News has no exact URL; all six requested Reddit communities return HTTP 403; indexed public X search yields no original post. These are retrieval gaps.

## Research judgment

- Priority: high. It makes graders, logs, cost aggregation, and versioned replay explicit objects of agent-evaluation measurement.
- Confidence: high. Scripts, derived records, correction history, and maintainer confirmation reinforce each other; scope is limited to two leaderboards and raw reruns are costly.
- Audience: agent benchmarks, leaderboards, trajectory datasets, cost analysis, harness, and reproducibility engineering.
- Watch: maintainer outcomes on tau-bench and tau2-bench issues, merged fixes, in-archive errata, broader independent audits, and licensing.
