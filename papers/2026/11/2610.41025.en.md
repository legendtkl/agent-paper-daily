## Conclusion summary

- ReplayRCA evaluates a failure diagnosis by inserting its replacement action into the original trajectory, letting the same worker continue, and checking whether the task succeeds. It uses neither human labels nor an LLM judge.
- The score subtracts a paired placebo that replays the worker's original action at the same step. This avoids crediting spontaneous recovery or restart effects to the diagnosis.
- Across 62 calibrated systematic failures, the best of 13 models reaches +51.3 points of causal repair, but none of 78 pairwise differences survives multiple-comparison correction. The observed leaderboard is not a stable hierarchy.
- Post-hoc diagnosis does not imply live prevention. On 87 supervised runs, GPT-6.1 Sol loses 14.5 points from post-hoc to live intervention and is the only jointly evaluated model with a significant gap.

## Research question and background

Most agent RCA benchmarks ask a model to identify a wrong step and compare the answer with a human or model label. Multiple repairs may be valid, and prior suites allow an always-last-step shortcut to score 67–96%. ReplayRCA replaces agreement with executable intervention: a proposed diagnosis earns credit only when its action changes the outcome.

The paper also separates two roles. An autopsy model sees a complete failed run and is told that it failed. A live supervisor sees an irreversible action before execution, does not know the eventual outcome, and may intervene once. Both tracks use the same environment, runs, and causal-effect score.

## Method

OpsWorld is a deterministic synthetic back office with 15 normal tools, three task families, and six difficulty levels; the last adds QR codes, photographed forms, and handwriting. Every world is seed-generated. Restoration replays all actions before the intervention and checks each observation byte for byte.

A diagnosis returns a step index and replacement tool call. The system restores the preceding state, applies the replacement, and runs the same worker five times. Causal repair is replacement continuation success minus original-action continuation success at that same step. Live supervision also charges five points per intervention and measures false alarms on runs that would have succeeded.

Candidate failures must fail in at least two of three fresh attempts. Calibration retains failure runs with at most 20% spontaneous success and success controls with at least 80% spontaneous success under original-action replay.

## Experiments and evidence

- The autopsy pool has 62 failures: 41 from DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, 11 from Claude Haiku 5.5, and 10 from GPT-6 Luna. Thirteen diagnoser models are compared.
- Grok 4.7 scores +51.3 points, with a roughly +40 to +63 95% bootstrap interval. The top eight are statistically inseparable, and five items are repaired by no model in any continuation.
- Forcing GPT-6.1 Sol to choose the last step reduces causal repair from +45.2 to +28.1. A consensus-step ranking has only 0.43 Spearman correlation with replay effects.
- Live oversight covers 87 runs: 62 heading for failure and 25 heading for success. Gemini 3.8 Flash and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash score +29.1 and +27.5 net points and are statistically tied.
- For the four models in both tracks, GPT-6.1 Sol drops from +45.2 post-hoc to +30.6 live, a -14.5-point gap with `p = 0.002`; the other three intervals include zero.
- The complete study used 66,074 API calls, 6,258 worker continuations, about 822 million input tokens, and 141 million output tokens. The paper discloses that vendor reasoning settings are not fully matched.

## Limitations and risks

The 62 failures and 87 supervised runs leave intervals around plus or minus ten points. About 84 failures would be needed to distinguish models ten points apart with 80% power. Only one simulated environment is demonstrated, and two of its three task families dominate the pool.

Claude models ran at `high` rather than maximum effort, making cross-vendor rank comparisons unfair. One small worker dominates the pool, stronger-worker failures were expensive to harvest, and the protocol permits only one correction and one intervention.

API model drift and stochastic continuations remain. Most importantly for reproduction, the public repository omits the paper's item pool, diagnoses, and continuation caches. Local checks validate the generator, restoration, and score, not the reported leaderboard.

## Reproduction and application

This review pins commit `b0bfc87f5bb359b47585bfd6809611a4ce5b5c73`. Under Python 3.13, `python -m replayrca.selftest` validates 720 generated task instances, and `tests/test_scoring.py` passes synthetic scoring, oversight, and incomplete-cache checks. The OpenCV-based media check is skipped because OpenCV is not installed.

This does not independently reproduce the 62/87-item study, 13-model ranking, or 6,258 continuations. Paper-level data must be requested from the authors, and a full rerun would require roughly 963 million input/output tokens plus provider costs.

ReplayRCA fits coding agents, back-office agents, database workflows, and sandbox tasks with restorable state and executable checkers. Systems without exact rollback can use it for offline replay and incident review, but not as an online causal guarantee.

## Related work

- [Who&When](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00212) and successors evaluate labeled error steps; ReplayRCA uses executable consequences instead.
- [CausalFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25338) validates generated repairs by intervention; ReplayRCA turns replay into a common score for arbitrary diagnosers.
- [SymTrace](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01546) shows that unguided reruns may recover; ReplayRCA therefore subtracts a same-step original-action placebo.
- [BLINDSPOT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16305) studies runtime responses for long-horizon tool agents; ReplayRCA permits one replacement action and scores its realized effect.
- [Prefix Executability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40444) measures first failure, full executability, and recovery; ReplayRCA tests whether a diagnosis changes the outcome.

## Research judgment

- Reading priority: high. It replaces plausible failure explanation with outcome-grounded intervention.
- Confidence: medium-high. Pairing, restoration, bootstrap, and multiplicity correction are strong; small samples, unmatched effort, and unavailable result data limit independent verification.
- Relevant to: agent evaluation, runtime oversight, incident analysis, reversible workflows, and safety engineering.
- Watch next: public caches, more environments and stronger workers, human-expert controls, multi-step intervention, and real irreversible side effects.
