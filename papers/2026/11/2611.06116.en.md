## Conclusion summary

- The study holds four models fixed and varies five harnesses: pipeline, tool loop, policy-by-search, long memory, and same-model reviewer. Pooled correctness spans only 4.8 points, but the largest within-model spread is 11.5 points and model rankings reverse.
- The same-model reviewer reduces violations from 9.6% to 6.4% (`p<0.001`) without a detectable increase in over-refusal, at roughly 1.8 times the input-token cost of the ordinary tool loop.
- The 11.5-point result is not a universal law. The formal model-by-harness interaction is only suggestive (`p=0.07`), and one model has no detectable harness effect.
- In 26.1% of 287 violating episodes, both judges agree that the final message denies an action that was actually executed. Conversation-only evaluation misses execution-layer violations.
- The released artifacts permit complete offline re-scoring of all 3,120 traces. This run reproduced the analysis without calling the four models again.

## Research question and background

Banks often validate a model but deploy a system composed of the model, context assembly, tool interfaces, memory, and reviewers. Benchmarks that fix the harness or publish one score per model cannot separate model capability from retrieval, context, process control, and review failures.

The paper narrows this into a controlled experiment: keep cases and models fixed, change only the harness, and measure violations, over-refusals, and correct outcomes under a synthetic banking policy. Its 40-section, 1,746-word manual maps 11 rule families to European conduct provisions and explicitly identifies controls that are stricter than the underlying law.

## Method

The simulated bank exposes 13 state-changing tools and six lookup tools. Its 156 preregistered cases comprise 39 variants, two replicates, and two pressure conditions. Four open-weight models run each of five harnesses, producing 3,120 two-turn episodes. A deterministic scorer reads executed traces and sent messages; no LLM judge enters the compliance score.

H0 is a structured pipeline, H1 a ReAct-style tool loop, H2 exposes policy only through search, H3 adds a long benign customer memory, and H4 adds a same-model action reviewer. The protocol was amended once after a pilot but before the main run; both preregistration hashes are released. Paired tests, Cochran's Q, and GEE assess harness and interaction effects. A separate stated-versus-executed analysis uses two model-family judges and a blind human sample.

## Experiments and evidence

Pooled correct-case rates are 90.5% for H0, 88.8% for H1, 87.3% for H2, 88.5% for H3, and 92.1% for H4. qwen3.8-flash-next moves from 82.1% under H2 to 93.6% under H4, an 11.5-point spread; deepseek-v4.1-flash spans only 1.9 points. The GEE model-by-harness interaction is `χ²(12)=19.8, p=0.07`.

H4 versus H1 yields 6.4% versus 9.6% violations, with 4 versus 24 discordant pairs (`p<0.001`); over-refusal is 1.4% versus 1.6% (`p=1.00`). Long memory raises violations from 9.6% to 11.1% (`p=0.02`). Under H2, retrieval failures have 17.1% violations versus 10.3% when a relevant section is retrieved (Fisher `p=0.05`), although H2 versus H1 overall is not significant (`p=0.10`).

Failures concentrate in date arithmetic. The reviewer improves unauthorized-transaction cases but not the 14-day withdrawal window, illustrating that a reviewer sharing the same capability gap does not repair it.

At the pinned commit, this run passed all 22 scorer tests, re-scored all 3,120 raw episodes, and ran the hypothesis, stated-versus-executed, and human-agreement analyses. Key rates and tests match the paper. This validates the released traces and analysis, not token-level reproduction of model calls.

## Limitations and risks

The bank, customers, and policy are synthetic; some controls are stricter than law. The study covers four medium-sized open models on one research API with reasoning disabled. Seven of 11 rule families are near ceiling outside H2, the interaction misses the 5% threshold, and the scripted second customer turn may contribute to the denial pattern.

The strongest conclusion is not that one harness is always safer, but that safety scores belong to a model-harness pair. H4's benefit should not be generalized without its 1.8-times input cost, shared-model blind spots, and the synthetic policy boundary.

## Reproduction and application

The repository releases all 3,120 raw traces, 156 cases, policy, five harnesses, deterministic scorer, preregistration hashes, judge labels, 100 blind human labels, and analysis scripts. Code is MIT; data and documents are CC BY 4.0. Offline analysis requires Python 3.11+, pandas, SciPy, statsmodels, and openpyxl.

Re-running model calls requires the research API or compatible endpoints and will not produce identical traces across providers or model versions. Useful engineering practices are to version the harness, score action logs, report violations and over-refusals together, and ablate retrieval, memory, and reviewers separately.
