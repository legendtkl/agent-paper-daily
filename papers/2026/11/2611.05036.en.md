## Conclusion summary

- ToolSpace reverses the usual synthesis order: it first mines data, state, and retry dependencies from real MCP and agent traces, then clusters environments, samples executable workflows, and reverse-generates tasks and assertions.
- Across seven long-horizon tool benchmarks, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B rises from a 23.0 average to 41.0, or 43.2 after agent RL. The 744B checkpoint averages 56.6, equal to Claude Opus 5 under this unweighted aggregation.
- Seven frontier models lose score on AutomationBench Pro and Tau3-Banking Pro; the largest AutomationBench drop is 38.2 points.
- A central boundary is that 77.8% of tools and 77.5% of environments are stateless or non-operational and replay cached returns. This verifies cache consistency, not current executability against live services.

## Research question and background

Most executable-environment pipelines synthesize a database and scenario before deriving tools and tasks. That ordering can produce interfaces unlike real APIs, compositions without execution evidence, and tasks trapped inside one environment.

ToolSpace treats tools as atomic capabilities and uses observed value flow, object state, and retry behavior as composition evidence. The goal is to scale the space of witnessed tool compositions rather than to hand-build one complete simulated world.

## Method

Deterministic rules create typed edges between adjacent calls. A data edge carries a type-compatible output into the next input; a state edge links calls that operate on the same object; a retry edge captures a failed call followed by a successful alternative. An LLM does not decide whether an edge merely looks plausible.

The weighted graph is clustered into environments. Operational tools receive restorable state, query tools read that state, and stateless tools return recorded outputs. State-aware walks require every argument to trace to the task, initial state, or an earlier observation and roll back on failure.

Successful chains are decompiled into natural-language goals plus state, trace, and output assertions. A sample survives only if it replays from the initial snapshot. The resulting data supports SFT and outcome-grounded agent RL, which motivates the `AT` primary category.

## Experiments and evidence

- The paper analyzes 21,385 trajectories with at least five calls. 66.2% span at least two environments, average length is 18.86 calls, and edge shares are 69% data, 25% state, and 6% retry.
- Qwen3-8B moves from 7.2 to 19.6, or 20.7 with RL; Qwen3.6-35B-A3B moves from 23.0 to 41.0, or 43.2 with RL; GLM-5.2 moves from 44.6 to 56.6.
- Replacing witnessed edges with LLM-judged edges drops the 35B model from 45.8 to 37.8 on AutomationBench and from 34.0 to 26.7 on Tau3-Banking. Removing edge types, rollback, or weights also lowers all five reported ablation benchmarks.
- The public AutomationBench Pro package contains 300 frozen tasks across six domains and 4,002 assertions. Tau3-Banking Pro contains 98 tasks. All seven tested frontier models score below their standard-split results.
- At commit `9d172585e42256d4d680f3dd7018c104d3460f45`, all 11 Tool-Space tests pass, one five-call cross-service fixture is generated and replay-verified, and all 300 AutomationBench Pro contracts, 4,002 assertions, and 11 checksummed files validate.

## Limitations and risks

The full ToolSpace graph is not released; the public implementation focuses on 47 AutomationBench services and 758 endpoints. Full training data, per-run results for the reported three repeats, and complete training costs are absent, so the main table cannot be independently rebuilt from this release.

For the stateless majority, execution and replay are cached lookups. The paper does not report live-service spot checks, walk or replay retention rates, or all graph-pruning, clustering, and walk-length thresholds. It reports averages over three runs but no dispersion. The 56.6 tie also averages heterogeneous native benchmark metrics and is not a statistical equivalence result.

The original `uv.lock` cannot install under Python 3.13 with `--locked` in this review; tests pass only after regenerating the lock. No root license covers the whole repository.

## Reproduction and application

This review validates code, an offline fixture, and AutomationBench Pro contracts. It does not train the models, call Boyue or commercial models, or run all seven online suites; it is an artifact and protocol check, not a main-experiment rerun.

A low-cost audit starts with the offline Tool-Space fixture and `Automation-Pro/evaluate.py validate`. Full evaluation needs Linux, Node.js, Docker or Podman, several model and search endpoints, and gated datasets. Full training additionally depends on unreleased graph and training assets.

The most reusable mechanisms are witnessed composition edges, argument provenance, rollback, and replay from an initial snapshot under deterministic assertions. Cache-backed tools should be labeled with provenance and freshness so cache-consistent is never reported as live-executable.

## Related work

- [ToolBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16789) scales real-API instruction data; ToolSpace adds an execution-derived composition graph and cross-environment workflows.
- [AppWorld](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18901) provides executable multi-app worlds; ToolSpace studies how to expand environments from traces.
- [tau-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045) and [tau2-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07982) evaluate stateful conversational tool use; ToolSpace creates a harder Banking Pro variant.
- Agent-World and MAGNET generate executable environments; ToolSpace first extracts deterministic relations from observed execution.
- [Trajectory2Gym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33568) turns trajectories into trainable environments; ToolSpace emphasizes a global tool graph and cross-environment state dependencies.

## Research judgment

- Priority: high. It presents a complete mechanism for scaling long-horizon executable training environments, not just another benchmark.
- Confidence: moderately high. External benchmarks, three scales, ablations, and artifacts are strong; cached replay, unreleased training data, absent variance, and licensing remain material gaps.
- Audience: tool-use training, MCP environments, agent data synthesis, long-horizon evaluation, and executable verification.
- Watch: full training assets, per-run results, live cache-backed audits, exact thresholds, a root license, lockfile repair, and third-party main-result reruns.
