## Selected papers

### [What Makes a Good Agent-Designed Library Interface?](../papers/2026/10/2609.36730.en.md)

- arXiv:2609.36730 | 95/100 | Primary [`EV`](../docs/categories.en.md#ev); secondary [`SE`](../docs/categories.en.md#se), [`TU`](../docs/categories.en.md#tu).
- LibraryDesignBench uses 15 library-design tasks, four languages, and 242 expert-validated problems to measure whether a library designed by one agent helps downstream agents write correct, simple programs. Opus 5.5 scores 48.9 versus 46.6 for production libraries; library limitations are the primary cause in 82% of 810 partially correct but overlong solutions.
- Selection rationale: the controlled two-stage design, cross-model users, production-library baseline, task-clustered intervals, open code, and local tests form a strong evidence chain. At a pinned commit, 212 tests passed and eight opt-in slow end-to-end tests were skipped; the 2,178 model evaluations per design configuration were not rerun.

### [Why Agents Still Fail After Checking When Tools Silently Lie](../papers/2026/10/2609.37153.en.md)

- arXiv:2609.37153 | 92/100 | Primary [`SS`](../docs/categories.en.md#ss); secondary [`EV`](../docs/categories.en.md#ev), [`TU`](../docs/categories.en.md#tu), [`RA`](../docs/categories.en.md#ra).
- ToxicBench preserves source data while corrupting observations returned from successful tool calls. In the 118-task, three-adapter GPT matrix, task success falls by 26–39 percentage points. On Claude Haiku 4.5, ordinary Double-pass reaches 1.00 poisoned success versus 0.90 for Verification-only, showing why retry budget must be separated from verification policy.
- Selection rationale: paired poisoning, transient and persistent conditions, an ordinary retry baseline, 200 newly human-labeled trajectories under a frozen grader, and open audit artifacts jointly support the claims. After installing a minimal compatible environment, 223/224 tests passed locally; the remaining figure-layout check lacked Times New Roman, while the official lockfile still has a Pillow conflict.
