### [Regularizing Recursive Self-Improvement of Agent Harnesses](../papers/2026/09/2609.24972.en.md)

- arXiv:2609.24972 | 98/100 | primary [`LT`](../docs/categories.en.md#lt); secondary [`AF`](../docs/categories.en.md#af), [`SY`](../docs/categories.en.md#sy), [`EV`](../docs/categories.en.md#ev).
- RRSI adds edit budgets, history-driven exploration, a critic, and a pruner to both proposal and selection in harness evolution. Across eight benchmarks it improves the evolution set by up to 14.1 points and five OOD benchmarks by up to 4.7 points, while using about 30% fewer policy tokens than unregularized evolution.
- Selection reason: it directly addresses overfitting in recursive harness improvement with three-domain OOD evaluation, component ablations, cost accounting, and public code. A full independent replay is still missing.

### [Measuring and Training Taste from Long-Horizon Agent Trajectories](../papers/2026/09/2609.25804.en.md)

- arXiv:2609.25804 | 96/100 | primary [`EV`](../docs/categories.en.md#ev); secondary [`RP`](../docs/categories.en.md#rp), [`AT`](../docs/categories.en.md#at), [`SE`](../docs/categories.en.md#se).
- Taste-Bench turns long-horizon trajectories into 502 decision forks; the best model reaches only 59.7% order-consistent accuracy. Outcome-aware teacher distillation raises a student from 30.0% to 47.9% on unseen tasks and improves a fixed executor from 14.6% to 33.7% on 41 SWE-bench Pro tasks.
- Selection reason: it converts terminal outcomes into trainable key decisions and includes human review, position-bias controls, and endpoint validation. Labels still depend on observed trajectories, and the 41-task endpoint study limits generalization.
- Freshness: v1 first submitted 2026-09-22 and v2 revised 2026-09-23; announced in the 2026-09-23 batch. This is a catch-up review after authentication recovery.
- Verifiable attention, checked 2026-09-26 09:03–09:18 Asia/Shanghai: 137 Hugging Face Daily Papers votes and zero comments; 11 GitHub stars, two forks, zero open issues; 30 dataset downloads and zero likes; no exact Hacker News hit.
- [Original arXiv page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25804); [independent Lark study](https://bytedance.larkoffice.com/docx/NrErd1FfgoKRtuxktPycT5WGnPe).

### [Can Agents Reproduce the Core Claims of Machine-Learning Papers?](../papers/2026/09/2609.28850.en.md)

- arXiv:2609.28850 | 95/100 | primary [`EV`](../docs/categories.en.md#ev); secondary [`RA`](../docs/categories.en.md#ra), [`SE`](../docs/categories.en.md#se), [`SS`](../docs/categories.en.md#ss).
- RECLAIM has four agents each reproduce 100 NeurIPS 2025 papers, creating 400 agent–paper cells. Auditing confirms only 73 reproductions. Failed runs consume just 29% of budget on average; major failures include testing the wrong artifact, running the wrong experiment, reimplementing without checking the paper's number, and stopping early.
- Selection reason: frozen tasks, GPU budgets, graders, run bundles, and per-run traces make real research-agent failures auditable. Single trials, no same-cluster human control, and LLM auditing constrain interpretation of the absolute rates.
- Freshness: v1 first submitted 2026-09-23 23:41:50 UTC, with no later revision; announced in the 2026-09-25 batch, the latest available batch.
