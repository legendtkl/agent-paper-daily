## Selected papers

### [Tool Availability Does Not Mean Tool Adoption](../papers/2026/08/2608.03327.en.md)

- arXiv:2608.03327 | 87/100 | Primary [`CU`](../docs/categories.en.md#cu); secondary [`TU`](../docs/categories.en.md#tu), [`SY`](../docs/categories.en.md#sy), [`AT`](../docs/categories.en.md#at)
- Under one GUI-MCP harness, tool injection raises a reasoning model by 4.0 points but lowers a non-reasoning model by 5.9 points; the benefiting model still covers only 23.9% of tool-reachable tasks. Dense rewards increase adoption without held-out accuracy, while matched-observation compression reaches 37.8% at about 53% of rich input cost.
- Selection rationale: 309 tasks, five-run estimates, diagnostics, and multi-turn RL probes clear the strong-paper threshold. Missing dedicated code, absent held-out gains, and no third-party response are explicit deductions. This is rolling-window paper eight and clears the >=85 exception threshold.

### [Does Persistent Experience Actually Improve Agents?](../papers/2026/08/2608.04003.en.md)

- arXiv:2608.04003 | 91/100 | Primary [`EV`](../docs/categories.en.md#ev); secondary [`LT`](../docs/categories.en.md#lt), [`KM`](../docs/categories.en.md#km), [`AF`](../docs/categories.en.md#af)
- PAST-Bench uses 26 task families, 204 fresh-session episodes, and matched persistence controls to report both later-task gains and save-retrieve-update evidence. Hermes+ raises the overall gap from +0.13 to +0.15 and mechanism evidence from 0.64 to 0.73, but Procedural regresses and tokens rise about 2.5 times.
- Selection rationale: controlled comparisons across seven models and four frameworks, mechanism experiments, costs, and open code provide strong evidence. Synthetic tasks and non-causal pathway attribution are the main limits. This is paper nine, clears 85, and does not duplicate the hybrid GUI-tool paper.
- Independent Lark note: <https://bytedance.larkoffice.com/docx/PMPTduqAMolM2Vxkc24cxlwsnQh>
