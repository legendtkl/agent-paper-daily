## Selected papers

### [How Long Can Coding Agents Maintain Their Own Code?](../papers/2026/11/2611.07166.en.md)

- arXiv 2611.07166; score 96; primary [`EV`](../docs/categories.en.md#ev), secondary [`SE`](../docs/categories.en.md#se) and [`SY`](../docs/categories.en.md#sy).
- Sixty-nine tasks chain 574 steps into persistent maintenance trajectories. The strongest system solves 18.5% end to end despite passing 56.8% of individual steps, so local success does not establish long-term delivery.
- This run checked task structure and 2,483 public score records without rerunning model trajectories. Complete agent messages and patch traces are not public.

### [Prompt Injection Can Tamper with Results Without Replacing the Task](../papers/2026/11/2611.07640.en.md)

- arXiv 2611.07640; score 94; primary [`EV`](../docs/categories.en.md#ev), secondary [`SS`](../docs/categories.en.md#ss) and [`TU`](../docs/categories.en.md#tu).
- The benchmark uses 90 cases and 1,433 variants to test silent changes to objectives, plans, actions, observations, and state. DRIFT-isolation lowers mean ASR from 17.0% to 6.6% while retaining 93.3% attacked TSR.
- Official lightweight tests pass. The community CarryoverGate effort covers only a 30-case pilot, with a small ASR change and substantial clean-sample intervention, so it is not a replication.
