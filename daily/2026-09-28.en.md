## Selected paper

### [Measuring Agent Exploration in Verifiable Alien Worlds](../papers/2026/09/2609.30199.en.md)

- arXiv:2609.30199 | 90/100 | primary [`EV`](../docs/categories.en.md#ev); secondary [`RP`](../docs/categories.en.md#rp), [`RA`](../docs/categories.en.md#ra).
- The paper builds AlienCode and AlienLogic, executable sandboxes with prior-conflicting rules, and evaluates ten systems over four rounds of autonomous probing, rule discovery, and held-out transfer. AlienCode's best Best@3 rises from at most 15.7% before exploration to 87.6%, versus 0.5%–11.0% without feedback; same-system trajectories differ by up to 72.8 points.
- Selection reason: interpreters and proof checkers provide exact outcomes, six conditions separate feedback from probe ownership, and trajectory- and budget-level reporting shows that discovering rules is not the same as applying them reliably. Private tasks and only three trajectories per system limit external validity and independent reproduction.
- Freshness: v1 was first submitted 2026-09-24 17:37:14 UTC and has no later revision; it entered the 2026-09-25 announcement batch and remains inside this run's 72-hour review window.
- Verifiable attention, checked 2026-09-28 09:04–09:18 Asia/Shanghai: 12 Hugging Face Daily Papers votes; no exact Hacker News hit; no official GitHub repository found. Third-party technical explainers exist, but no independent rerun was found.
- [Original arXiv page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30199); [independent Lark study](https://bytedance.larkoffice.com/docx/I6wJdyPY0oHH0vxxUlBchY5UnMd).
