# Agent Papers Monthly Review | 2026-03

[中文](2026-03.md) | English

> Type: historical retrospective. This review was completed on 2026-08-02 and assigns papers to months by the first submission date shown on official arXiv pages and APIs. The entries are a curated set directly related to AI agents, not all preprints from the month, and are not presented as results of a daily selection process that was running at the time.

## Scope and Count

- Period: 2026-03-01 to 2026-03-31.
- Selected: 25 papers, within the target range of 20–35 papers per month.
- Areas covered: tool use and training, memory, agent runtimes, coding agents, computer use, evaluation, and safety.
- Metadata basis: each paper's arXiv abstract page and official API. Dates use the first submission date, and titles preserve the official spelling.
- Technical-claim boundary: core contributions summarize abstracts only. Results in abstracts are treated as author-reported, not independently reproduced findings.

## Monthly theme

**Agent research began to treat execution itself as a trainable, observable, and governable system object.**

This month was not limited to comparisons of model capability or final success rate. Evaluation moved down to steps, constraints, and auditor reliability; systems work treated scheduling, context, processes, and harnesses as distinct design layers; and memory research shifted from “retain more” toward retrieval bottlenecks, control-flow risks, and active forgetting. Meanwhile, tool training increasingly emphasized executable verification, data diversity, and environment stability.

## Trend analysis

### 1. Evaluation Moved from Final Answers to Steps, Constraints, and Evaluators

ASTRA-bench, AgentProcessBench, and CCTU respectively add personal-context complexity, human step-level labels, and executable constraint checks. CUAAudit goes further by measuring the accuracy, calibration, and disagreement of automated auditors. The change is not merely that benchmarks are longer, but that they must answer where a failure occurs, whether constraints were violated, and whether the judge itself is reliable.

### 2. Harnesses and Runtimes Became an Independent Research Layer

OPENDEV, AgentRM, Quine, and AgentOS discuss the system around the model at different levels. The first two provide implementations and system metrics, Quine attempts to reuse POSIX process semantics, and AgentOS proposes a broader operating-system agenda. The common shift is that scheduling, context compression, isolation, and state lifecycles are no longer treated as incidental prompt details.

### 3. Memory Research Shifted from “What to Store” to “When to Read and How to Control”

The retrieval-bottleneck study suggests that retrieval choice may matter more than sophisticated writing in current pipelines; MemoryCD moves evaluation to years of cross-domain behavior; Oblivion explicitly controls reading, writing, and decay. At the same time, MEMFLOW shows that persistent memory can alter tool control flow, indicating that utility and safety need to share the same memory-governance interface.

### 4. Agent Training Depended on Executability, Diversity, and Environment Stability

CoVe binds data generation to verification through constraints, DIVE derives tasks from real tool trajectories, and the long-horizon tool-RL study treats environment stability as a central axis. Training data was no longer valued only for more instructions; trajectories increasingly had to be executable, outcomes inspectable, and structural distributions controllable.

### 5. Computer Use Shifted Toward Professional Software, Continuous Video, and Reusable Skills

OSExpert acquires procedural skills through environment exploration, CUA-Suite provides continuous human demonstrations in professional applications, and video reward modeling judges outcomes directly from execution videos. These works respectively contribute skill acquisition, training data, and outcome supervision, showing that the unit of GUI-agent research is expanding from individual clicks to complete temporal tasks.

## Evidence and reproducibility limits

- The 25 papers this month are a thematic selection rather than a complete bibliometric result; counts should not be interpreted directly as topic popularity.
- Experimental scale and performance figures in the table come from paper abstracts and have not been independently reproduced item by item. Formal citation requires checking task settings, baselines, and statistical methods in the paper body.
- arXiv papers may continue to be revised. Titles, code status, and results should follow the latest version at access time; month assignment on this page remains fixed to the first submission date.
- AgentOS and Bootstrapping Coding Agents are notably conceptual or case-based. Their value lies primarily in identifying system boundaries and design hypotheses and should not be interpreted on the same evidentiary level as multi-benchmark empirical work.

## Follow-up

- Whether process evaluation develops cross-environment error taxonomies and judge-calibration protocols.
- Whether harnesses progress from empirical implementations to explicit state contracts, reversible changes, and repeatable systems benchmarks.
- Whether memory forgetting, deletion, and privacy governance jointly demonstrate retained utility and recovery from attacks.
- Whether professional computer-use evaluation adds repeated runs, human baselines, and cross-application tasks rather than only increasing screenshots or task counts.
