## Trend analysis

### 1. Verifiers are becoming core components of agent systems

DeepSWE, DynamicMCPBench, PAIChecker, and OSReward respectively evaluate functional implementation, environment effects, dataset pairing, and reward models. The common change is that benchmark tests, task pairings, and judges are no longer assumed to be inherently reliable. Evaluation must state who built the verifier, which valid paths it accepts, how it may misclassify outcomes, and how far it is from real task success.

### 2. Training and the real execution shell are converging

OpenForgeRL trains directly in real harnesses, SEED extracts skills from the current policy's trajectories, DeepSearch-World uses deterministic environments for self-distillation, and Frontis-MA1 connects executable MLE feedback to program evolution. These works reduce structural differences between training and deployment, but also import environment exploits, harness bias, and runtime cost into the optimization objective.

### 3. Long-horizon capability is no longer described by success rate alone

Long-Horizon-Terminal-Bench records intermediate progress and execution cost; AREX preserves verified evidence and unresolved constraints; Qwen-UI-Agent, ABot-World-0, and Metis respectively address cross-device workflows, visual-world drift, and persistent state. Taken together, the month's work indicates that long-horizon agents need to report progress, state, recovery, resources, and terminal outcomes.

### 4. Agent infrastructure becomes an object of research and training

Harness Handbook maps behavior to code, DataFlow-Harness constrains agents to produce editable platform artifacts, and DynamicMCPBench generates outcome checkpoints on live services. Infrastructure is no longer merely an implementation detail; it is an experimental variable that directly affects capability, reproducibility, and safety.

### 5. Safety evaluation shifts from fixed attack sets to adaptive stress testing

Adaptive Adversaries shows that multi-turn attacks substantially increase success, while GuardianAgentBench places safeguards at structured checkpoints before tool execution. Both support the direction that model-level prompt defenses cannot carry safety responsibility alone, although scenario counts, attack budgets, and safeguard costs still constrain generalization.

- In historical collection records, ABot-World-0, AskChem, Qwen-UI-Agent, Metis, and Harness Handbook had relatively high Hugging Face interaction; ABot-World-0 also had relatively high GitHub attention. The old records lack a consistent snapshot time, so the figures cannot support strict cross-paper ranking.
- SEED, OpenForgeRL, Metis, Frontis-MA1, DeepSWE, and OSReward explicitly state in the paper or abstract that code, models, data, or benchmarks are released. Reproduction still requires checking license terms, versions, and completeness.
- Where no original community link and collection time were preserved, this review does not add claims such as “widely accepted” or “controversial.” Institutional reputation, paper recency, and interaction counts do not substitute for technical validity.

## Evidence and reproducibility limits

- These 20 papers come from a curated source set and do not represent the complete arXiv distribution. Direction shares should not be interpreted directly as field-wide research popularity.
- Experimental figures come from paper abstracts or verified standalone research notes. All remain author-reported rather than independently reproduced.
- Scores across benchmarks are not directly comparable because environment versions, model harnesses, budgets, and definitions of success differ.
- MANTA, PAIChecker, and OSReward were initially submitted at the end of July and received standalone research notes on August 1, but are included in this retrospective by initial submission month.

## Follow-up

- Whether independent teams can verify the misclassification rates of verifiers, reward models, and handwritten tests.
- Whether reinforcement learning inside a harness transfers across harnesses, models, and environments instead of depending on a specific engineering implementation.
- Whether long-running tasks report tokens, wall-clock time, retry counts, recovery rates, and failure paths together.
- Whether native memory, world models, and real GUI agents can preserve state consistency and safety boundaries in open environments.
