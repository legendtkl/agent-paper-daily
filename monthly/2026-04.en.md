# Monthly Agent Paper Review | 2026-04

[中文](2026-04.md) | English

> Type: retrospective monthly review. This retrospective was completed on 2026-08-02. Papers are assigned to months by the initial submission date shown on the official arXiv page/API. The entries are a curated set directly related to AI agents, not a complete list of the month's preprints, and the retrospective is not presented as if the papers had been selected by the daily automation at the time.

## Scope and Volume

- Period: 2026-04-01 to 2026-04-30.
- Selected papers: 24, within the monthly target range of 20–35.
- Topics covered: memory governance, tool training and protocols, agent runtimes, coding agents, computer use, deep research, evaluation, and safety.
- Metadata source: each paper's arXiv abstract page and the official API. Dates use initial submission dates, and titles retain their official wording.
- Standard for technical claims: core contributions summarize only the paper abstract. Results stated in an abstract are treated as author-reported, not as independently reproduced findings.

## Monthly theme

**The focus shifted from “can an agent complete the task?” to “can the environment, harness, verifier, and permission system jointly support reliable execution?”**

This month brought larger and longer computer-use and tool-use benchmarks, as well as evaluations of production coding agents, real professional software, and reliability across repeated runs. Runtime research also began to define more explicit contracts for state, scheduling, and harnesses. Safety work expanded from static sandboxing to least-capability sets, backdoored tools, and harms that emerge over long action sequences.

## Trend analysis

### 1. As environments scale, evaluation shifts toward task realism and repeat-run reliability

Gym-Anything expands coverage to 200 software packages and more than 10,000 tasks, while FrontierFinance and WindowsWorld add expert-level long tasks and cross-application professional workflows. At the same time, On the Reliability of Computer Use Agents requires repeated execution of the same task. Benchmark scale now includes not only the number of tasks, but also human effort, trajectory length, application composition, and variance across runs.

### 2. Verifiers are no longer treated as error-free infrastructure

Universal Verifier examines rubrics, process rewards, and context management for long trajectories; AgentProp-Bench measures judge agreement against a human subset; IntentScore uses action-level rewards for runtime reranking. These works address outcome judgment, judge calibration, and pre-action choice respectively, showing that training rewards, leaderboards, and online recovery share the same verifier-reliability problem.

### 3. Harnesses move from implicit loops to explicit state and observable modification

ClawVM defines minimum-fidelity and write-back constraints for context and persistent state, the structured-graph harness separates planning, execution, and recovery, and AHE requires every harness change to carry a testable prediction. Compared with March's runtime exploration, April places greater emphasis on state contracts, attribution, and rollback conditions rather than merely proposing new agent loops.

### 4. Tool research advances toward both unified representations and empirical counterevidence

UniToolCall unifies training and evaluation representations, COVERT introduces noise and ambiguity while preserving the Oracle, and GTA-2 moves from atomic calls to open-ended workflows. The Tool Illusion warns that tool gains may depend on models, frameworks, and benchmark settings. Tool count, call success, and real task completion should be reported separately.

### 5. The safety boundary expands beyond container isolation to capabilities, memory, and long sequences

AgentHazard evaluates harm accumulated through locally reasonable actions, Back-Reveal exfiltrates data through combined memory and tool use, Aethelgard attempts to reduce capabilities by task, and Secure Forgetting tests whether deleted information resists inference. The shared direction is to complement static sandboxing with explicit capability and information-flow constraints.

## Evidence and reproducibility limits

- These 24 papers are a topic-focused selection, not a complete bibliometric result; paper counts should not be interpreted directly as topic popularity.
- Experimental scales and performance figures in the table come from paper abstracts and have not been independently reproduced item by item. Before formal citation, consult the full text for task settings, baselines, failure cases, and statistical methods.
- arXiv papers may continue to be revised. Titles, code status, and results should be checked against the latest version at the time of access; month assignment on this page remains fixed to the initial submission date.
- From Agent Loops to Structured Graphs is explicitly a position and design proposal. The abstract of Beyond Static Sandboxing also does not provide empirical evidence commensurate with the scope of its framework. Their evidence strength should not be treated as equivalent to that of public, large-scale benchmarks.

## Follow-up

- Whether larger environments provide stable images, version pinning, resettable state, and training interfaces with manageable cost.
- Whether automated judges report agreement with humans, calibration error, and false-positive/false-negative rates across failure types.
- Whether automatic harness optimization retains gains across repositories, models, and tasks, while recording harmful changes and rollbacks.
- Whether least privilege, state deletion, and tool-supply-chain defenses can form a composable and auditable runtime policy.
