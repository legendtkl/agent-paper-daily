#### [Agent in a Bottle: Can LLM Agents Turn Their Capabilities Into Cheap, Scalable Artifacts?](../papers/2026/10/2610.08775.en.md) | [2610.08775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08775)

- Selection date: 2026-10-08; primary: [`EV`](../docs/categories.en.md#ev); secondary: `SY`, `AF`, `RA`; score: 93/100.
- Core contribution: 1. The paper proposes "bottling": When faced with millions of similar instances, the Agent does not call expensive models one by one. Instead, it independently trains small models, writes programs, or combines reusable artifacts within a fixed time, computing power, and API budget. 2. BOTTLED measured this capability using 3 million workloads, 10 models, and unified OpenCode Harness. Each model-task is run 2 times, for a total of 60 main runs; each time is up to 10 hours, 1 A100 40GB, 8 CPU, 64GB RAM and weighted 5 million tokens. 3. General task knowledge is not equal to encapsulation ability: 48/60 times bottling is lower than the lower bound of the corresponding zero-shot 95% confidence interval, 31/60 times is inferior to the naive small model distillation baseline with the same token budget, and 21/60 times exhausts the budget. 4. The upper limit of capabilities is still considerable. Opus 5 retains about 82% of the zero-shot macro-F1 on ESCI and reports about 657 times lower cost; however, this success only occurs on some models and tasks and cannot be generalized to general cost reduction. 5. High priority for reading. The paper separately measures the completion of Agent's one-time tasks and "converting capabilities into repeatable, low-margin-cost workpieces." However, there are currently only 3 types of classification tasks and 2 Agent runs per grid. The public warehouse also lacks pollution audit and Jev evaluation code.

## Trend analysis

### 1. Evaluation is separating task success, evidence, process, and harness behavior

September brought tool-schema, delivery-constraint, and cost audits. October goes further by decomposing success into evidence acquisition, trace integrity, harness mechanisms, recovery, and business outcomes. ToxicBench, SpecGAP, TraceTrial-Bench, FDE-Gym, and AuthorizationBench show that task completion can coexist with false evidence, lost requirements, harness failures, unchanged customer metrics, or unauthorized action. A terminal score alone is increasingly insufficient.

### 2. The harness is becoming trainable, generatable, and evolvable

September mostly examined harness components and interface representation. ActiveSaddler, Doc2Harness, Harness-Policy Optimization, RSIHub, and The Trinity now select training tasks, generate harnesses from specifications, jointly optimize policy and scaffolding, and organize recursive improvement. This also creates attribution problems: oracle routing, self-routing, weight updates, and harness updates must be reported separately.

### 3. Training uses real execution feedback, exposing verifier boundaries

DepGPO, ReTerm, PPRS, Data World, and Trajectory2Gym turn file dependencies, residual failure state, world-state distance, generative mechanisms, or observation-action structure into training signals. Compared with September's emphasis on cost and tool interfaces, October more directly anchors feedback in executable environments. The limits are equally clear: verifier-invisible effects, simulator bias, single training runs, and in-domain evaluation can make local gains look general.

### 4. Safety extends from static refusal to learning, clarification, and authorization

SafeActBench, AuthorizationBench, and AgentDrift cover pre-action evidence, authorization contracts, and post-deployment learning. Failures can arise from benign rewards, replacement by a similar skill, or unnecessary autonomous action rather than an overtly malicious prompt. Safety therefore needs a record of authorization source, clarification timing, execution boundary, and subsequent learning state.

### 5. Reproducibility is shifting from open code to recomputable artifacts and versioned environments

The stronger October evidence packages include frozen data, traces, verifier scripts, pinned commits, or recomputable tables. Context Pipeline also received a third-party offline recomputation. Most papers still depend on commercial models, dynamic pages, real devices, or unavailable traces. An open repository does not establish main-experiment reproduction; static inspection, author-script recomputation, focused tests, environment replay, and independent reruns remain distinct claims.

## Community and open-source observations

Verifiable material mainly comes from Hugging Face, GitHub, Pith, Paperium, technical review sites, and author project pages. Much of it is AI-generated review or secondary explanation. It can surface methodological objections but cannot replace peer review or an independent rerun. Cairn Commons' offline recomputation of Context Pipeline is one of the few explicitly labeled third-party computational checks, and its scope remains limited to mechanical claims in the public package.

The recurring consensus is that model, harness, and verifier results should be reported separately; real environments and long traces improve external validity but reduce reproducibility; and cost, permission, and provenance belong in result interpretation. Disputes center on single-run evidence, closed judges, simulator bias, mixing oracle and deployable configurations, and treating engagement as quality. Repeated Reddit HTTP 403 responses and sparse exact matches on Hacker News or public X are retrieval and sampling gaps, not evidence that no discussion exists.

## Evidence and reproducibility limits

- All 43 papers have an independent research note and a community-response section, but original discussion links, collection times, and engagement verification are uneven. Missing elements remain explicit evidence gaps.
- Most experimental results are author-reported. Compilation, unit tests, table recomputation, or public-trace audits validate only the inspected artifact, not the complete headline experiment.
- Commercial models, live web pages, physical devices, and online services drift across versions and regions. Cross-paper comparison requires model snapshots, harnesses, tool budgets, verifiers, and environment versions.
- The 43 papers are curated daily selections, not a complete arXiv sample. Their count and shares do not estimate field-wide volume or growth.
